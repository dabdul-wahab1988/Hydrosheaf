# HydroSheaf: an auditable evidence-integration framework for groundwater age, connectivity and reaction inference with controlled-synthetic validation

Groundwater interpretation often combines hydraulic, tracer and hydrochemical evidence even though the evidence streams can be incomplete, structurally discrepant and non-identifying. HydroSheaf was implemented as a modular Python framework for retaining candidate connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic explanations while calibrating uncertainty, recording model discrepancy, abstaining from unsupported commitments and evaluating the value of a next measurement. The software was evaluated under a locked controlled-synthetic programme in which independent simulators generated observations from known sealed states that were withheld until scoring. The programme comprised three generator families, 12 development cases and six held-out cases, with missingness, censoring, permutation controls and truth-blind prospective policy selection. Age coverage including abstention was 0.9655; specialist mean absolute error was 4.24 years versus 7.65 years for the competence-matched baseline. Reaction-family log loss was 0.896 versus 2.852, with coverage 0.739 and false commitment 0.038. Kinetic prediction passed with root-mean-square error 0.119 and interval coverage 1.0, but overall parameter identification was only 0.167 because the rate constant k and reactive surface area A were structurally confounded through k×A; parameter estimates were withheld when independent surface-area information was absent. The integrated policy passed its bounded cost-adjusted utility rule, with mean utility per cost 1.478 versus 0.246 for random, while a general-superiority claim remained withheld. No general topology-superiority gate was passed. Application to 320 Northern Ghana seasonal records, 41 Lower Anayari records and 63 Talensi records demonstrated data-readiness and chemistry-level diagnostics, not field validation of age, directed connectivity or unique reactions. HydroSheaf therefore supports a bounded synthetic-performance and auditable workflow claim, not universal groundwater-inference superiority.

## 1. Introduction

Groundwater interpretation is an inverse problem in which the state of interest is only indirectly observed. Hydraulic heads constrain potential gradients, environmental tracers constrain mixtures of recharge histories and residence times, and dissolved chemistry records the cumulative effects of mixing, mineral dissolution, precipitation, exchange and redox. These evidence streams are complementary in principle, but they are not interchangeable. A stable isotope can support recharge or mixing plausibility without establishing groundwater age; a chemistry fit can close a mass balance without proving that the proposed edge is a physical flow path; and a particle-tracking path can be conditional on the conceptual and parameterised flow model that generated it. Data-limited aquifers therefore require a method that reports what the observations constrain, what remains compatible, and when an apparently precise answer is unsupported.

The problem is not simply the absence of a sophisticated solver. Groundwater age distributions are model-dependent and can be non-unique when tracer information is sparse or when alternative lumped-parameter models fit the same observations [@jurgens2012tracerlpm; @mccallum2015limitations]. Multiple age tracers can improve constraint, but the resulting estimate remains conditional on recharge histories, mixing assumptions and the treatment of measurement and model error [@visser2013multitracer]. Likewise, conventional inverse hydrogeochemical tools, including PHREEQC, are powerful tools for speciation, reaction-path and inverse calculations, but a best-fitting reaction set is not automatically a unique reaction history [@parkhurst2013phreeqc]. Environmental modelling literature has long described this equifinality: structurally different explanations can reproduce the same limited observations [@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto].

The same issue applies to the use of coupled numerical models. MODFLOW 6 and MODPATH 7 provide established mechanisms for simulating groundwater flow and particle trajectories [@langevin2017modflow6; @pollock2016modpath], but agreement between a reduced-order network and a model-generated path is conditional on the model, boundary conditions and parameterisation. A public age dataset containing fitted lumped-parameter model outputs is also a reference-emulation resource, not independent truth. This distinction matters because complete confirmation of a natural-system model is not available from a finite set of observations; model evaluation must remain relative to an explicit purpose and test domain [@oreskes1994validation].

HydroSheaf was initially assembled as a workflow linking graph-based connectivity, residence-time inference and sparse inverse hydrogeochemistry. This study documents a substantive upgrade in what is being evaluated. The production package now contains specialist candidate generation, calibrated age and reaction baselines, a regularised adjusted plus-minus (RAPM) reaction layer with on/off evidence scoring, explicit model-discrepancy calibration, discrete model averaging, a kinetic adapter that can expose structural `k×A` confounding, selective-risk metrics, abstention records and truth-blind cost-adjusted measurement selection. The programme contract reports PASS when all declared criteria are met, WEAK when a finite result falls below a performance floor, and ABSTAIN when the software withholds a result or claim because it is unsupported, non-identifiable or not comparable. The contribution is consequently an executable evidence and claim architecture. It is not presented as a replacement for MODFLOW, MODPATH, TracerLPM or PHREEQC, and the “sheaf” terminology is used as sheaf-inspired evidence integration rather than as a claim of a new formal sheaf theorem [@robinson2017sheaves; @hansen2019spectral].

A second design problem is comparator fairness. An integrated method can appear to improve simply because it receives a larger candidate universe, a more informative input panel or a privileged view of the synthetic state. The present study therefore treats candidate generation as part of the comparator, records the observation contract for every result, and separates development calibration from held-out scoring. Specialist methods are not reduced to a single number: age is judged by coverage and selective risk as well as error, reaction by probability quality and false commitment as well as log loss, and kinetics by prediction separately from parameter identification. This makes both positive and negative results interpretable.

The central question was:

> Can a modular evidence-integration framework combine connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic evidence while calibrating uncertainty, representing disagreement, abstaining when information is insufficient and producing reproducible decisions within a declared synthetic domain?

Five objectives followed. First, the implemented package boundary and data flow were documented. Second, age, reaction-family and kinetic components were compared with competence-matched specialist baselines under held-out synthetic conditions. Third, model discrepancy and model averaging were tested as safeguards against treating incompatible interpretations as one precise answer. Fourth, the next-measurement policy was evaluated prospectively with truth hidden until after selection and with explicit measurement cost. Fifth, the real Ghanaian data were audited to determine which workflow outputs were supportable and which claims remained non-identifiable.

The expected contribution was therefore conditional. A PASS result on an independent synthetic case would support a bounded software-performance statement under the generator and observation mechanisms tested. It would not establish universal superiority, field effectiveness or complete physical truth. Conversely, an abstention or a failure signal would be a scientific output because it identifies where the evidence contract refuses to overstate an interpretation. This framing follows the broader principle that calibration and predictive behaviour should be evaluated separately from apparent fit, and that uncertainty should include model inadequacy rather than only parameter variation [@kennedy2001calibration; @brynjarsdottir2014discrepancy].

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Software architecture and evidence representation

HydroSheaf version 0.5.1 was treated as the production software surface. The package provided a public end-to-end network-inference entry point and validation tools for metrics, candidate generation, specialist baselines, calibration, discrepancy, model averaging, decision utility and claim gates. The source repository was separated analytically into reusable package code, benchmark and validation control scripts, historical analysis records and machine-readable run artefacts. Only the first category was treated as reusable production code; the other categories documented experimental controls or evidence rather than additional package capabilities.

The runtime represented groundwater observations as nodes, candidate directed edges and evidence attached to nodes or edges. A candidate edge was not interpreted as an observed flow path. Hydraulic direction, tracer compatibility, chemical residuals, thermodynamic limits and temporal information contributed separate evidence records. Candidate sets and compatible outcome sets were retained until the claim gate, so that a conflict could be reported as model disagreement, numerical non-convergence, non-identifiability or abstention. The production pipeline staged end-member preparation, temporal processing, environmental-tracer age inference, sheaf-style consistency refinement and network fitting. Validation components could also be executed separately to prevent a successful specialist calculation from being mistaken for a successful integrated run.

### 2.2 Specialist comparators and calibration

The age comparator generated its own truth-blind candidate universe rather than accepting HydroSheaf's selected edges. Candidate age distributions varied mean age, mixing shape and atmospheric-history assumptions, with optional multi-tracer and excess-air corrections. Candidate likelihoods were evaluated only from supplied observations and their declared uncertainty. Development cases were used to fit calibration parameters; locked cases were scored without access to sealed truth. Age outputs were reported as intervals or abstentions. Coverage, mean interval width, relative width, acceptance rate and selective risk were calculated together, because a narrow interval with poor coverage or a high abstention rate is not a successful age engine. A competence-matched baseline was scored using the same observation contract and candidate target.

Reaction inference used two independent layers. A stoichiometric and thermodynamic candidate generator enumerated reaction-family explanations from the observed chemistry, including a null or no-supported-family option. The RAPM layer then represented reaction-family evidence using regularised adjusted plus-minus coefficients with on/off terms for evidence channels. RAPM is project-specific notation for this scoring layer, not a claim that an established external groundwater method uses the name. Calibration was fitted on development cases using blocked case splits, temperature scaling and classwise reliability checks. Locked outputs were assessed with multiclass log loss, coverage, classwise expected calibration error, selective risk and false commitment. A selected reaction family was therefore a calibrated family-level report, not proof of a unique mineral phase or causal field process. Sparse L1 and elastic-net structure was retained as a parsimonious component, with thermodynamic direction constraints where the declared chemistry supported them [@tibshirani1996lasso; @zou2005elasticnet; @parkhurst2013phreeqc].

### 2.3 Discrepancy, averaging and abstention

When hydraulic, age and chemical components produced different predictions for the same target, a discrepancy calibrator estimated the scale required to cover development residuals. The scale was applied before model averaging, rather than treating disagreement as independent extra evidence. Discrete model weights were fitted on development observations only by case-blocked log score. The averaging report retained the component distributions, pairwise total-variation disagreement and the compatible outcome set. A frozen total-variation threshold of 0.25 marked material disagreement. The simplex Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) residual and objective stopping diagnostics determined convergence. The raw unconstrained gradient norm was retained as a diagnostic but was not interpreted as a simplex stationarity residual. This distinction prevented a flat or boundary-constrained optimisation record from being described as a perfectly identified physical model [@kennedy2001calibration; @brynjarsdottir2014discrepancy; @neuman2003bma].

Unsupported likelihoods, missing required fields and incompatible model outputs were written as auditable failure or abstention records before interval arithmetic or policy ranking. This was essential for selective-risk reporting. An abstention was not counted as a correct prediction; it reduced coverage and was reported alongside conditional risk. False commitment meant that the software issued a committed interpretation incompatible with the sealed outcome under the declared outcome set. The distinction between marginal coverage and coverage after selection follows the logic of selective and conformal prediction, but the present intervals were calibrated only within the declared synthetic programme [@angelopoulos2023conformal]. The claim gates required calibration, held-out generators, mechanism or family stratification, finite outputs and a maximum false-commitment rate. The integrated gate additionally required calibrated discrepancy, converged averaging, complete independent post-measurement outcomes and a truth-blind policy selector.

Comparator inputs were matched before any score was calculated. Each specialist received the same observed variables, uncertainty conventions, missingness pattern and candidate-action costs available to HydroSheaf for the corresponding task. A baseline could use its own candidate-generation rule, but it could not receive sealed truth, HydroSheaf-selected candidates or a post hoc edge correction. The random policy was evaluated over the same feasible actions and costs as the learned policies. Negative controls were retained as part of the contract: permutation of tracer, chemistry or head associations should remove case-specific signal, while structured missingness and censoring tested whether the methods degraded or abstained in a declared way. These controls distinguish a genuine inference advantage from an advantage created by leakage or by an easier input representation.

### 2.4 Kinetic and next-measurement components

The kinetic specialist used the production PHREEQC-compatible kinetic adapter and a controlled forward benchmark with held-out kinetic regimes. Predictive concentrations and an effective rate were scored separately from parameter identification. The parameter pair consisted of a rate constant (k) and reactive surface area (A). When the forward law depended on their product, additional residence-time observations did not by themselves identify both parameters. The benchmark therefore reported conditional identification when independent surface-area information was supplied, overall identification, parameter abstention, interval coverage, parameter error and predictive root-mean-square error. The `k×A` confounding was a required report, not a post-hoc explanation.

The decision-utility layer represented candidate measurements, costs, scenario beliefs and expected information or declared utility. Prospective policies received only candidate actions and pre-measurement distributions. Hidden states and benefits were released only after all policies had selected or abstained. Policy performance was scored as utility minus cost, with paired bootstrap contrasts against random and specialist policies. Bayesian experimental-design theory motivated the cost-aware structure, but the current result was interpreted only within its declared synthetic utility contract [@chaloner1995design].

### 2.5 Controlled-synthetic programme and claim gates

The locked integration programme used three generator families: an independent analytic lattice, an independent multilayer mixing generator, and an independent MODFLOW 6/MODPATH 7 generator coupled to nonlinear chemistry and tracer equations. The generators did not import HydroSheaf. Twelve cases were used for development and six cases were held out for final scoring. Generator source hashes, executable hashes and observation-stress metadata were recorded. Stress conditions included structured missingness, left censoring, combined missingness and censoring, tracer permutation, chemistry permutation and head permutation. Calibration, thresholds and policy tuning were restricted to development cases. The final inference received observations only; sealed truth was opened for scoring after prediction or action selection. A versioned analysis plan archived these rules before assembly, but it was not an external preregistration.

Age, reaction, kinetics and integrated performance were evaluated by separate predeclared gates. The gates required finite metrics, held-out evidence, calibration and an explicit claim scope. The integrated gate did not convert positive paired utility into a claim of universal superiority. A separate explicit superiority gate remained withheld, so the allowed wording was bounded controlled-synthetic decision performance.

The gate thresholds were interpreted jointly rather than as a licence to optimise one metric. The age contract required target coverage of 0.95, a relative interval-width ceiling of 1.5, selective risk no greater than 12 years and a baseline error comparison; its minimum acceptance rate was 0.5. Reaction required coverage of at least 0.25, maximum classwise expected calibration error no greater than 0.35, false commitment no greater than 0.10 and selective risk no greater than 0.4, together with a log-loss comparison. Kinetics required predictive RMSE no greater than 0.25, parameter error no greater than 0.50, interval coverage of at least 0.90 and an identification rate of at least 0.8 among locked cases with an independent surface-area measurement; the overall unidentified fraction was reported separately. The integrated contract required six or more locked prospective cases, calibrated discrepancy, model-averaging convergence and no false commitment. These thresholds were safety floors for a bounded programme claim, not evidence that the associated component was optimal. Passing a threshold did not erase the domain restrictions, case count or dependence on the declared generator family.

### 2.6 Real field-data application

The field application used the project field-data archive. The Northern Ghana workbook contained Dry and Wet sheets with 160 wells each and 29 variables, giving 320 seasonal records. Lower Anayari contained 41 records, and Talensi contained 63 records. Independent charge-balance screening classified Northern Ghana as 294 quantitative, 19 screening and 7 exploratory records. Lower Anayari had one censored fluoride value and no blank cells; Talensi had nine missing redox-potential values and no fluoride, strontium or silica. A coordinate-neighbour field runner generated candidate edges and chemistry fits; these were treated as candidate-screening outputs, not measured hydraulic paths. The field datasets were read alongside the source-study descriptions for Northern Ghana, Anayari and Talensi [@anku2008ghana; @zakaria2021anayari; @chegbeleh2020talensi].

No field dataset contained an environmental age-tracer panel, screen-top and screen-bottom intervals, repeated hydraulic-head time series, independently verified directed connectivity, mineralogical reaction truth or a validated coupled aquifer model. Stable isotopes were used only for recharge, source or mixing plausibility. Field results were therefore reported as data-readiness, chemistry-transfer, reaction-family and non-identifiability diagnostics. They were not included in the synthetic performance gates.

### 2.7 Provenance and statistical reporting

Results were read from machine-readable run records and field audit records. Paired uncertainty intervals used the declared 95% bootstrap procedure with 2,000 resamples where available. For the integrated policy, the resampling unit was the six locked cases, so the interval was a within-run descriptive uncertainty summary rather than a confidence interval over all possible aquifers or generator families. The provenance manifest recorded a generating source revision, cryptographic hashes for source files and whether source changes had been committed. Because the run was generated before all source changes were committed, its reproducibility status remained provisional and a rerun from a committed source tree is required. Full candidate schemas, reaction templates, thresholds and regeneration details are provided in Supplementary Methods.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Implemented package and validation surface

The installed package loaded successfully as HydroSheaf version 0.5.1. The available modules contained a public network-inference pipeline and domain tools for graph construction, transport, temporal processing, nuclear tracers, PHREEQC-compatible reaction blocks, sheaf-style topology refinement and active learning. The validation tools contained the age and reaction specialist candidate generators, RAPM calibration, calibrated intervals, discrepancy calibration, discrete model averaging, kinetic benchmarking, prospective policy evaluation, generator-quality audits and programme-level gates. These components were therefore present in software rather than existing only in manuscript plans. Benchmark protocols, historical analyses and evidence archives were kept distinct from the reusable package code.

The locked integration execution gate was PASS. All required stages were complete, no runtime errors were recorded, the independent generator-quality audit was PASS, stress-test records were complete, and the external MODFLOW 6/MODPATH 7 execution gate was PASS. The run contained 12 development and six held-out cases from three independent generator families. Eighteen generator-quality records reported no blocker or major finding. The provenance record retained source hashes and the generating revision, but also recorded that source changes had not yet been committed, which limited the strength of the reproduction claim.

The component boundaries were visible in the import and artifact audits. Age and reaction comparators had independent candidate-generation entry points; calibration, discrepancy and model-averaging outputs were stored separately from the public network pipeline; and the kinetic adapter exposed prediction and parameter-identification statuses as different fields. There was no top-level topology-superiority result in this run. The prior-assisted topology diagnostic was not pooled with the independent specialist comparisons because its prior came from a model-generated path structure. This separation prevented a software inventory from being mistaken for evidence that every component had already met a general benchmark.

### 3.2 Age component

The calibrated age gate passed on the locked cases. Coverage including abstention was 0.9655, mean interval width was 18.64 years and relative width was 0.5445. Selective risk was 2.10 years at an acceptance rate of 0.9655. The competence-matched specialist MAE was 4.24 years compared with 7.65 years for the baseline, and the baseline non-inferiority condition was retained. Held-out generator and family-stratified checks passed. These results support calibrated synthetic age inference under the declared atmospheric-history, tracer and observation mechanisms. They do not show that an age distribution inferred from the Ghanaian field isotope columns is correct, because those columns do not contain environmental age tracers.

### 3.3 Reaction-family component

The reaction gate was evaluated on 142 locked specialist records. The RAPM/on-off layer reduced multiclass log loss to 0.8964 from 2.8520 for the competence-matched baseline. Coverage was 0.7394, maximum classwise expected calibration error was 0.2710, selective risk was 0.2190 and false commitment was 0.0385. Held-out generator and mechanism-stratified requirements passed. The result was consequently stronger than the earlier fixed-comparator diagnostic, but its estimand remained reaction-family classification under a declared dictionary and evidence contract. It did not establish a unique mineral reaction, prove a causal source, or establish universal superiority to PHREEQC or other specialist reaction-path tools.

### 3.4 Kinetic component

The held-out kinetic gate passed for predictive performance. Predictive root-mean-square error was 0.1191, reported parameter error was 0.0781 and calibrated interval coverage was 1.0. Conditional identification was 1.0 among cases supplied with an independent surface-area observation, but overall identification was only 0.1667 and the parameter-abstention rate was 0.8333. This pattern was the intended scientific output: the production adapter could predict the response and recover parameters when an independent measurement broke the structural confounding, while it refused to report unrestricted (k) and (A) values when only their product was identified. Additional residence-time observations alone did not remove the `k×A` confounding.

### 3.5 Discrepancy, model averaging and integrated decision utility

The integrated run recorded calibrated discrepancy, converged model averaging, complete independent post-measurement outcomes and zero observed false commitment under the locked gate. HydroSheaf's mean cost-adjusted utility per cost was 1.4778, compared with 0.2464 for the uniform random policy and −0.0039 for the strongest specialist comparator. The paired 95% lower bounds for the HydroSheaf difference were 0.9941 versus random and 1.2208 versus the specialist. These values indicated positive bounded utility on the six locked synthetic cases under the declared simulator, action costs and benefit function. Because the six locked cases were the resampling units, these lower bounds were descriptive within-run intervals and not population-level uncertainty intervals.

The result was not converted into a universal superiority statement. The prospective benchmark therefore retained an abstention from a general-superiority claim, because a positive utility contrast under six generator-realised cases is not a general comparison with all groundwater-inference engines. The correct result was a bounded prospective synthetic decision diagnostic. Model-averaging artefacts also retained both the simplex KKT residual and the raw unconstrained gradient diagnostic. KKT and objective criteria passed the run's convergence contract, while the raw gradient norm was 0.786 against a nominal raw-gradient tolerance of (10^{-5}). This was not treated as evidence of perfect optimisation; it was retained as a follow-up diagnostic and the integrated claim was kept bounded.

### 3.6 Field-data application and evidence ceiling

The real-data inventory contained 320 Northern Ghana seasonal records from 160 repeated wells, 41 Lower Anayari records and 63 Talensi records. Northern Ghana had 294 quantitative, 19 screening and seven exploratory records after independent charge-balance screening. Lower Anayari had no blank cells and one censored fluoride value. Talensi had nine missing redox-potential values and no fluoride, strontium or silica. None of the three datasets contained environmental age tracers, screen intervals, independently verified directed connectivity or reaction truth.

The field chemistry workflow generated 82 Lower Anayari and 126 Talensi candidate edge records, 208 in total. The overall median chemistry R², or coefficient of determination, was approximately 0.713, with site medians of 0.600 and 0.766. These were in-sample candidate-edge chemistry closures, not held-out prediction and not proof of physical connectivity. The broader field-transfer audit showed the same evidence ceiling. In Northern Ghana, all 160 wells were reported as partially identifiable at the maximum chemistry tier, with approximately 7.1 reaction alternatives retained on average rather than a single best fit. Removing strontium and silica increased the non-identifiable fraction from 0.6% to 51.9%, while the mean resolution score changed little. Three internally generated edge sets differed by total-variation distances of 0.12 and 0.05 from the chemistry-neighbour reference, demonstrating network sensitivity.

The seasonal hold-forward diagnostic found that graph-ridge prediction improved on persistence by 0.173 log1p units, 95% confidence interval [0.158, 0.188], but its advantage over the expanding mean-delta baseline was 0.0051 with interval [−0.0017, 0.0119]. The field application therefore demonstrated ingestion, quality screening, chemistry prediction and measurement-value diagnostics. It did not validate a field flow network, groundwater age, unique reaction mechanism or coupled digital twin.

The field evidence also constrained what could be carried into a future prospective design. The Northern Ghana workbook supplied repeated wet/dry labels but not a timestamped temporal sequence, so the hold-forward result was a seasonal transfer diagnostic rather than a chronological forecast. The Lower Anayari and Talensi edge records were generated from coordinate-neighbour rules and closed against the same chemistry used to fit them; their R² summaries were therefore evidence of numerical fit and candidate sensitivity. The field datasets remained valuable as real-data stress cases because they reveal missing age tracers, omitted surface chemistry variables and charge-balance heterogeneity that a future sampling design must address.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What the current result establishes

The central result was that HydroSheaf had become an executable, claim-gated software framework rather than only a collection of coupled algorithms. A specialist comparator generated its own candidates, calibration was fitted before locked scoring, model disagreement was retained, and the integrated policy was required to make a truth-blind decision before synthetic outcomes were released. This is a stronger scientific object than a post-hoc comparison of fitted values. It makes the failure modes of the inference chain measurable: insufficient coverage, excessive interval width, false commitment, non-identifiability, model disagreement, incomplete outcomes and missing provenance can each stop a claim.

The bounded synthetic results were encouraging. Age and reaction-family components passed their declared gates against competence-matched baselines, and kinetic prediction passed while parameter abstention exposed the structural limit created by k×A. Integrated utility was positive against random and non-inferior to the strongest specialist under the locked decision rule. These are meaningful results because they combine independent generator families, held-out cases, adverse observation patterns and calibrated reporting. They are not evidence that every HydroSheaf output is correct, nor that the method will dominate specialist tools in an arbitrary aquifer. In numerical Earth-science modelling, a successful agreement between a model and a chosen synthetic truth is inherently conditional on the model purpose and test domain [@oreskes1994validation].

The comparison is strongest where the data contract is explicit. HydroSheaf was not allowed to borrow the specialist's candidate set, and the specialist was not penalised for declining to infer a quantity absent from its own observation contract. Conversely, the six locked cases are not a random sample of all possible aquifers: they are a test of the declared generator families and stress mechanisms. The appropriate interpretation is therefore evidence of competence and safe behaviour within a controlled domain, followed by a request for broader held-out testing. This is also why the manuscript reports both positive contrasts and unpassed domains. A package that reports its own evidence ceiling is more useful for field deployment than one that converts every component score into a single ranking.

### 4.2 Why the result is not a universal groundwater-inference claim

HydroSheaf and specialist tools solve different problems. MODFLOW 6 represents groundwater flow through a numerical discretisation and boundary-condition framework; MODPATH traces particles through that flow field; TracerLPM fits lumped age-distribution models to environmental tracers; and PHREEQC provides thermodynamic, reaction-path and inverse-geochemical calculations [@langevin2017modflow6; @pollock2016modpath; @jurgens2012tracerlpm; @parkhurst2013phreeqc]. HydroSheaf adds a layer for retaining candidate evidence, comparing specialist outputs, quantifying disagreement and deciding when not to collapse alternatives. That layer can be valuable without replacing the specialist forward models. The current evidence does not establish topology superiority, because the full programme gate is focused on age, reaction-family, kinetics and integrated policy diagnostics and the topology component has no single general-superiority gate. Earlier prior-assisted topology scores must likewise be separated from no-prior inference, because supplying a MODPATH-derived prior is not an independent comparison.

The reaction result illustrates the distinction between predictive skill and mechanism truth. RAPM improved log loss substantially and kept false commitment low, which indicates that calibrated family-level probabilities were more useful than fixed comparators under the tested cases. The result still depended on a declared reaction dictionary, stoichiometric constraints, evidence channels and calibration population. Equifinal reaction sets can remain compatible even when a family score is well calibrated [@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto]. A field label such as “silicate weathering” or “denitrification” therefore remains a hypothesis unless supported by independent mineralogical, redox, tracer or process evidence. The field chemistry R² values have the same limitation: they show that a candidate residual can be fitted, not that the candidate edge or reaction history occurred.

The age result similarly demonstrates why calibrated intervals are necessary. Specialist MAE and coverage improved under the synthetic atmospheric-history and tracer contract, but groundwater age is known to depend on input histories, mixing structures, screening and tracer limitations [@jurgens2012tracerlpm; @mccallum2015limitations]. The stable (^{18})O and (^{2})H columns in the Ghana workbook can support source or mixing plausibility, but they cannot substitute for tritium, radiocarbon, CFC, SF6, noble-gas or other environmental age evidence. The study therefore reports synthetic age competence and refuses a field-age claim.

### 4.3 Why abstention and failure signals are scientific outputs

An integrated system should not be rewarded merely for returning a precise answer. Model discrepancy was included because ignoring model inadequacy can produce biased and over-confident parameter estimates [@kennedy2001calibration; @brynjarsdottir2014discrepancy]. The same logic applies to candidate topology, reaction class and kinetic parameters. When component distributions differed beyond the declared threshold, HydroSheaf retained the mixture for audit but returned a model-disagreement abstention. When the kinetic data identified only k×A, the correct result was a prediction with parameter abstention. When a prospective superiority gate was not established, the correct statement was positive bounded utility with superiority withheld. Each decision converts a hidden failure into a record that can guide the next measurement.

This behaviour also limits a common statistical trap. A system can reduce entropy by imposing a strong prior or by allowing correlated evidence to be counted twice. A smaller interval is not proof of better inference. The current model-averaging result was therefore reported with the KKT residual, objective diagnostics and raw gradient diagnostic. The raw gradient norm was not treated as a contradiction of the KKT result because the optimisation was constrained to a simplex; nevertheless, its retention makes the numerical qualification visible and identifies a reproducibility task before a stronger averaging claim is made. The framework's value lies partly in making such disagreements inspectable.

The same principle explains why the reaction component is best described as an improved calibrated family diagnostic, not yet a complete reaction-inference engine. The lower log loss and low false-commitment rate show that the RAPM/on-off evidence layer improved probabilistic ranking for the tested dictionary. Coverage and classwise calibration still leave room for error, and the family dictionary cannot represent an unlisted process. An improved next version should add held-out reaction mechanisms, perturb the dictionary itself, include independent mineralogical or redox observations where available, and report per-family confusion and calibration. Those tests would challenge the component's scientific scope rather than merely tune its coefficients on the same synthetic mechanisms.

### 4.4 Relationship to the real Ghanaian data

The Ghana datasets are useful precisely because they expose the difference between field operation and field truth. HydroSheaf accepted heterogeneous chemistry, screened analytical balance, retained alternative reactions, quantified the value of strontium and silica, and showed that candidate network composition changed when the edge construction changed. Those are actionable outputs for deciding what to measure next. The seasonal result also showed why a strong baseline matters: graph-ridge was better than persistence but was not clearly better than an expanding seasonal mean-delta model. This prevents a visually plausible graph from being credited with an incremental predictive benefit that the data do not establish.

The present field application cannot resolve residence time, exact directed paths, screen-resolved vertical exchange or a unique reaction mechanism. Northern Ghana has repeated wet and dry records but no actual sampling dates within the canonical workbook, no independent graph and no age-tracer panel. Talensi is additionally charge-balance limited, while Lower Anayari lacks the strontium and silica measurements that the ablation identifies as load-bearing. These limitations are not a failure of the field datasets; they are the evidence ceiling that HydroSheaf is designed to expose. Established work on prediction in data-limited hydrology similarly treats transferability and degradation under missing information as scientific questions rather than assuming that the best-resourced case represents every basin [@sivapalan2003pub; @hrachowitz2013pub].

### 4.5 Remaining limitations and way forward

The locked synthetic result used six test cases, so its uncertainty is dominated by generator-domain coverage rather than by a large random sample of all aquifers. More independent geometries, heterogeneity regimes, tracer histories, reaction mechanisms, analytical covariance structures and missingness mechanisms are needed. Reaction results should be reported per generator, family and case, not only as a pooled gate. Topology needs a dedicated component contract with strong hydraulic and particle-tracking baselines. The integrated policy needs more locked cases and a formal superiority or non-inferiority decision that is declared before scoring; the current positive utility result must remain bounded.

The next reproducibility action is a rerun from a committed source tree that records commit, environment, executable, generator and result hashes in one tracked manifest. The model-averaging raw-gradient diagnostic should be reconciled with the simplex KKT definition. The public package should also be cleaned so historical adapters and scripts do not appear to be duplicate production implementations, and the validation namespace should be reduced or grouped without changing the scientific results. A final prospective field stage should then add environmental age tracers, screened intervals, repeated heads, independent connectivity evidence, mineralogical or redox observations and pre-registered next-measurement decisions. Until that stage is complete, the study supports a bounded controlled-synthetic performance claim and a field-readiness claim, not field validation or universal superiority.

Several upgrades are now concrete implementation requirements rather than manuscript aspirations. The clean rerun should be generated from a committed tree and should compare the exact result hashes with the current run. The topology component should receive a separate contract with an independent hydraulic-gradient baseline, a particle-tracking baseline and no-prior controls. The integrated policy should expand the locked case set, include held-out generators not used to shape the policy, and declare a superiority or non-inferiority rule before scoring. For reaction and age, the benchmark registry should make the candidate universe, calibration population and observation subset machine-readable so that future specialist additions are competence-matched by construction.

## 5. Conclusion

HydroSheaf was implemented as an auditable evidence-integration framework that links connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic components to calibration, model-discrepancy reporting, abstention and cost-aware measurement selection. The current controlled-synthetic programme passed its declared age, reaction-family, kinetic and integrated decision gates across three independent generator families and held-out cases. The result supports a bounded statement that the software can produce calibrated and failure-aware synthetic inferences under the tested observation mechanisms. It does not establish that HydroSheaf is the best groundwater inference engine, that it is universally superior to MODFLOW, MODPATH, TracerLPM or PHREEQC, or that it identifies unique field processes.

The kinetic result provides the clearest example of the intended behaviour: predictive performance was acceptable, but parameter identification was reported only when independent surface-area information broke the k×A confounding. The Ghana application likewise showed that HydroSheaf can expose data quality, measurement value, chemistry-prediction limits and network sensitivity while refusing unsupported age, connectivity and mechanism claims. The next required stage is a clean-commit synthetic rerun with wider held-out generator coverage, followed by prospective field sampling with independent age, head, screen and process evidence. The study therefore establishes how HydroSheaf was built and what its current software can support; it does not close the field-validation question.
