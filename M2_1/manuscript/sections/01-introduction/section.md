## 1. Introduction

Groundwater interpretation is an inverse problem in which the state of interest is only indirectly observed. Hydraulic heads constrain potential gradients, environmental tracers constrain mixtures of recharge histories and residence times, and dissolved chemistry records the cumulative effects of mixing, mineral dissolution, precipitation, exchange and redox. These evidence streams are complementary in principle, but they are not interchangeable. A stable isotope can support recharge or mixing plausibility without establishing groundwater age; a chemistry fit can close a mass balance without proving that the proposed edge is a physical flow path; and a particle-tracking path can be conditional on the conceptual and parameterised flow model that generated it. Data-limited aquifers therefore require a method that reports what the observations constrain, what remains compatible, and when an apparently precise answer is unsupported.

The problem is not simply the absence of a sophisticated solver. Groundwater age distributions are model-dependent and can be non-unique when tracer information is sparse or when alternative lumped-parameter models fit the same observations [@jurgens2012tracerlpm; @mccallum2015limitations]. Multiple age tracers can improve constraint, but the resulting estimate remains conditional on recharge histories, mixing assumptions and the treatment of measurement and model error [@visser2013multitracer]. Likewise, conventional inverse hydrogeochemical tools, including PHREEQC, are powerful tools for speciation, reaction-path and inverse calculations, but a best-fitting reaction set is not automatically a unique reaction history [@parkhurst2013phreeqc]. Environmental modelling literature has long described this equifinality: structurally different explanations can reproduce the same limited observations [@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto].

The same issue applies to the use of coupled numerical models. MODFLOW 6 and MODPATH 7 provide established mechanisms for simulating groundwater flow and particle trajectories [@langevin2017modflow6; @pollock2016modpath], but agreement between a reduced-order network and a model-generated path is conditional on the model, boundary conditions and parameterisation. A public age dataset containing fitted lumped-parameter model outputs is also a reference-emulation resource, not independent truth. This distinction matters because complete confirmation of a natural-system model is not available from a finite set of observations; model evaluation must remain relative to an explicit purpose and test domain [@oreskes1994validation].

HydroSheaf was initially assembled as a workflow linking graph-based connectivity, residence-time inference and sparse inverse hydrogeochemistry. This study documents a substantive upgrade in what is being evaluated. The production package now contains specialist candidate generation, calibrated age and reaction baselines, a regularised adjusted plus-minus (RAPM) reaction layer with on/off evidence scoring, explicit model-discrepancy calibration, discrete model averaging, a kinetic adapter that can expose structural `k×A` confounding, selective-risk metrics, abstention records and truth-blind cost-adjusted measurement selection. The programme contract reports PASS when all declared criteria are met, WEAK when a finite result falls below a performance floor, and ABSTAIN when the software withholds a result or claim because it is unsupported, non-identifiable or not comparable. The contribution is consequently an executable evidence and claim architecture. It is not presented as a replacement for MODFLOW, MODPATH, TracerLPM or PHREEQC, and the “sheaf” terminology is used as sheaf-inspired evidence integration rather than as a claim of a new formal sheaf theorem [@robinson2017sheaves; @hansen2019spectral].

A second design problem is comparator fairness. An integrated method can appear to improve simply because it receives a larger candidate universe, a more informative input panel or a privileged view of the synthetic state. The present study therefore treats candidate generation as part of the comparator, records the observation contract for every result, and separates development calibration from held-out scoring. Specialist methods are not reduced to a single number: age is judged by coverage and selective risk as well as error, reaction by probability quality and false commitment as well as log loss, and kinetics by prediction separately from parameter identification. This makes both positive and negative results interpretable.

The central question was:

> Can a modular evidence-integration framework combine connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic evidence while calibrating uncertainty, representing disagreement, abstaining when information is insufficient and producing reproducible decisions within a declared synthetic domain?

Five objectives followed. First, the implemented package boundary and data flow were documented. Second, age, reaction-family and kinetic components were compared with competence-matched specialist baselines under held-out synthetic conditions. Third, model discrepancy and model averaging were tested as safeguards against treating incompatible interpretations as one precise answer. Fourth, the next-measurement policy was evaluated prospectively with truth hidden until after selection and with explicit measurement cost. Fifth, the real Ghanaian data were audited to determine which workflow outputs were supportable and which claims remained non-identifiable.

The expected contribution was therefore conditional. A PASS result on an independent synthetic case would support a bounded software-performance statement under the generator and observation mechanisms tested. It would not establish universal superiority, field effectiveness or complete physical truth. Conversely, an abstention or a failure signal would be a scientific output because it identifies where the evidence contract refuses to overstate an interpretation. This framing follows the broader principle that calibration and predictive behaviour should be evaluated separately from apparent fit, and that uncertainty should include model inadequacy rather than only parameter variation [@kennedy2001calibration; @brynjarsdottir2014discrepancy].
