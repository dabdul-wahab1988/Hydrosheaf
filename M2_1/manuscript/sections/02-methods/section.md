## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Software architecture and evidence representation

HydroSheaf version 0.5.1 was treated as the production software surface. The package provided a public end-to-end network-inference entry point and validation tools for metrics, candidate generation, specialist baselines, calibration, discrepancy, model averaging, decision utility and claim gates. The source repository was separated analytically into reusable package code, benchmark and validation control scripts, historical analysis records and machine-readable run artefacts. Only the first category was treated as reusable production code; the other categories documented experimental controls or evidence rather than additional package capabilities.

The runtime represented groundwater observations as nodes, candidate directed edges and evidence attached to nodes or edges. A candidate edge was not interpreted as an observed flow path. Hydraulic direction, tracer compatibility, chemical residuals, thermodynamic limits and temporal information contributed separate evidence records. Candidate sets and compatible outcome sets were retained until the claim gate, so that a conflict could be reported as model disagreement, numerical non-convergence, non-identifiability or abstention. The production pipeline staged end-member preparation, temporal processing, environmental-tracer age inference, sheaf-style consistency refinement and network fitting. Validation components could also be executed separately to prevent a successful specialist calculation from being mistaken for a successful integrated run.

### 2.2 Specialist comparators and calibration

The age comparator generated its own truth-blind candidate universe rather than accepting HydroSheaf's selected edges. Candidate age distributions varied mean age, mixing shape and atmospheric-history assumptions, with optional multi-tracer and excess-air corrections. Candidate likelihoods were evaluated only from supplied observations and their declared uncertainty. Development cases were used to fit calibration parameters; locked cases were scored without access to sealed truth. Age outputs were reported as intervals or abstentions. Coverage, mean interval width, relative width, acceptance rate and selective risk were calculated together, because a narrow interval with poor coverage or a high abstention rate is not a successful age engine. A competence-matched baseline was scored using the same observation contract and candidate target.

Reaction inference used two independent layers. A stoichiometric and thermodynamic candidate generator enumerated reaction-family explanations from the observed chemistry, including a null or no-supported-family option. The RAPM layer then represented reaction-family evidence using regularised adjusted plus-minus coefficients with on/off terms for evidence channels. RAPM is project-specific notation for this scoring layer, not a claim that an established external groundwater method uses the name. Calibration was fitted on development cases using blocked case splits, temperature scaling and classwise reliability checks. Locked outputs were assessed with multiclass log loss, coverage, classwise expected calibration error, selective risk and false commitment. A selected reaction family was therefore a calibrated family-level report, not proof of a unique mineral phase or causal field process. Sparse L1 and elastic-net structure was retained as a parsimonious component, with thermodynamic direction constraints where the declared chemistry supported them [@tibshirani1996lasso; @zou2005elasticnet; @parkhurst2013phreeqc].

### 2.3 Discrepancy, averaging and abstention

When hydraulic, age and chemical components produced different predictions for the same target, a discrepancy calibrator estimated the scale required to cover development residuals. The scale was applied before model averaging, rather than treating disagreement as independent extra evidence. Discrete model weights were fitted on development observations only by case-blocked log score. The averaging report retained the component distributions, pairwise total-variation disagreement and the compatible outcome set. A frozen total-variation threshold of 0.25 marked material disagreement. The simplex Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) residual and objective stopping diagnostics determined convergence. The raw unconstrained gradient norm was retained as a diagnostic but was not interpreted as a simplex stationarity residual. This distinction prevented a flat or boundary-constrained optimisation record from being described as a perfectly identified physical model [@kennedy2001calibration; @brynjarsdottir2014discrepancy; @neuman2003bma].

Unsupported likelihoods, missing required fields and incompatible model outputs were written as auditable failure or abstention records before interval arithmetic or policy ranking. This was essential for selective-risk reporting. An abstention was not counted as a correct prediction; it reduced coverage and was reported alongside conditional risk. False commitment meant that the software issued a committed interpretation incompatible with the sealed outcome under the declared outcome set. The distinction between marginal coverage and coverage after selection follows the logic of selective and conformal prediction, but the present intervals were calibrated only within the declared synthetic programme [@angelopoulos2023conformal]. The claim gates required calibration, held-out generators, mechanism or family stratification, finite outputs and a maximum false-commitment rate. The integrated gate additionally required calibrated discrepancy, converged averaging, complete independent post-measurement outcomes and a truth-blind policy selector.

Comparator inputs were matched before any score was calculated. Each specialist received the same observed variables, uncertainty conventions, missingness pattern and candidate-action costs available to HydroSheaf for the corresponding task. A baseline could use its own candidate-generation rule, but it could not receive sealed truth, HydroSheaf-selected candidates or a post hoc edge correction. The random policy was evaluated over the same feasible actions and costs as the learned policies. Negative controls were retained as part of the contract: permutation of tracer, chemistry or head associations should remove case-specific signal, while structured missingness and censoring tested whether the methods degraded or abstained in a declared way. These controls distinguish a genuine inference advantage from an advantage created by leakage or by an easier input representation.

### 2.4 Kinetic and next-measurement components

The kinetic specialist used the production PHREEQC-compatible kinetic adapter and a controlled forward benchmark with held-out kinetic regimes. Predictive concentrations and an effective rate were scored separately from parameter identification. The parameter pair consisted of a rate constant (k) and reactive surface area (A). When the forward law depended on their product, additional residence-time observations did not by themselves identify both parameters. The benchmark therefore reported conditional identification when independent surface-area information was supplied, overall identification, parameter abstention, interval coverage, parameter error and predictive root-mean-square error. The `k×A` confounding was a required report, not a post-hoc explanation.

The decision-utility layer represented candidate measurements, costs, scenario beliefs and expected information or declared utility. Prospective policies received only candidate actions and pre-measurement distributions. Hidden states and benefits were released only after all policies had selected or abstained. Policy performance was scored as utility minus cost, with paired bootstrap contrasts against random and specialist policies. Bayesian experimental-design theory motivated the cost-aware structure, but the current result was interpreted only within its declared synthetic utility contract [@chaloner1995design].

### 2.5 Controlled-synthetic programme and claim gates

The locked integration programme used three generator families: an independent analytic lattice, an independent multilayer mixing generator, and an independent MODFLOW 6/MODPATH 7 generator coupled to nonlinear chemistry and tracer equations. The generators did not import HydroSheaf. Twelve cases were used for development and six cases were held out for final scoring. Generator source hashes, executable hashes and observation-stress metadata were recorded. Stress conditions included structured missingness, left censoring, combined missingness and censoring, tracer permutation, chemistry permutation and head permutation. Calibration, thresholds and policy tuning were restricted to development cases. The final inference received observations only; sealed truth was opened for scoring after prediction or action selection. A versioned analysis plan archived these rules before assembly, but it was not an external preregistration.

Age, reaction, kinetics and integrated performance were evaluated by separate predeclared gates. The gates required finite metrics, held-out evidence, calibration and an explicit claim scope. The integrated gate did not convert positive paired utility into a claim of universal superiority. A separate explicit superiority gate remained withheld, so the allowed wording was bounded controlled-synthetic decision performance.

The gate thresholds were interpreted jointly rather than as a licence to optimise one metric. The age contract required target coverage of 0.95, a relative interval-width ceiling of 1.5, selective risk no greater than 12 years and a baseline error comparison; its minimum acceptance rate was 0.5. Reaction required coverage of at least 0.25, maximum classwise expected calibration error no greater than 0.35, false commitment no greater than 0.10 and selective risk no greater than 0.4, together with a log-loss comparison. Kinetics required predictive RMSE no greater than 0.25, parameter error no greater than 0.50, interval coverage of at least 0.90 and an identification rate of at least 0.8 among locked cases with an independent surface-area measurement; the overall unidentified fraction was reported separately. The integrated contract required six or more locked prospective cases, calibrated discrepancy, model-averaging convergence and no false commitment. These thresholds were safety floors for a bounded programme claim, not evidence that the associated component was optimal. Passing a threshold did not erase the domain restrictions, case count or dependence on the declared generator family.

### 2.6 Real field-data application

The field application used the project field-data archive. The Northern Ghana workbook contained Dry and Wet sheets with 160 wells each and 29 variables, giving 320 seasonal records. Lower Anayari contained 41 records, and Talensi contained 63 records. Independent charge-balance screening classified Northern Ghana as 294 quantitative, 19 screening and 7 exploratory records. Lower Anayari had one censored fluoride value and no blank cells; Talensi had nine missing redox-potential values and no fluoride, strontium or silica. A coordinate-neighbour field runner generated candidate edges and chemistry fits; these were treated as candidate-screening outputs, not measured hydraulic paths. The field datasets were read alongside the source-study descriptions for Northern Ghana, Anayari and Talensi [@anku2008ghana; @zakaria2021anayari; @chegbeleh2020talensi].

No field dataset contained an environmental age-tracer panel, screen-top and screen-bottom intervals, repeated hydraulic-head time series, independently verified directed connectivity, mineralogical reaction truth or a validated coupled aquifer model. Stable isotopes were used only for recharge, source or mixing plausibility. Field results were therefore reported as data-readiness, chemistry-transfer, reaction-family and non-identifiability diagnostics. They were not included in the synthetic performance gates.

### 2.7 Provenance and statistical reporting

Results were read from machine-readable run records and field audit records. Paired uncertainty intervals used the declared 95% bootstrap procedure with 2,000 resamples where available. For the integrated policy, the resampling unit was the six locked cases, so the interval was a within-run descriptive uncertainty summary rather than a confidence interval over all possible aquifers or generator families. The provenance manifest recorded a generating source revision, cryptographic hashes for source files and whether source changes had been committed. Because the run was generated before all source changes were committed, its reproducibility status remained provisional and a rerun from a committed source tree is required. Full candidate schemas, reaction templates, thresholds and regeneration details are provided in Supplementary Methods.
