## 5. Conclusion

HydroSheaf was implemented as an auditable evidence-integration framework that links connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic components to calibration, model-discrepancy reporting, abstention and cost-aware measurement selection. The current controlled-synthetic programme passed its declared age, reaction-family, kinetic and integrated decision gates across three independent generator families and held-out cases. The result supports a bounded statement that the software can produce calibrated and failure-aware synthetic inferences under the tested observation mechanisms. It does not establish that HydroSheaf is the best groundwater inference engine, that it is universally superior to MODFLOW, MODPATH, TracerLPM or PHREEQC, or that it identifies unique field processes.

The kinetic result provides the clearest example of the intended behaviour: predictive performance was acceptable, but parameter identification was reported only when independent surface-area information broke the k×A confounding. The Ghana application likewise showed that HydroSheaf can expose data quality, measurement value, chemistry-prediction limits and network sensitivity while refusing unsupported age, connectivity and mechanism claims. The next required stage is a clean-commit synthetic rerun with wider held-out generator coverage, followed by prospective field sampling with independent age, head, screen and process evidence. The study therefore establishes how HydroSheaf was built and what its current software can support; it does not close the field-validation question.
