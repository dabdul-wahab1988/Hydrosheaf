# HydroSheaf: an auditable evidence-integration framework for groundwater age, connectivity and reaction inference with controlled-synthetic validation

Groundwater interpretation often combines hydraulic, tracer and hydrochemical evidence even though the evidence streams can be incomplete, structurally discrepant and non-identifying. HydroSheaf was implemented as a modular Python framework for retaining candidate connectivity, residence-time, reaction and kinetic explanations while calibrating uncertainty, recording model discrepancy, abstaining from unsupported commitments and evaluating the value of a next measurement. The software was evaluated under a locked controlled-synthetic programme in which independent simulators generated observations from known sealed states that were withheld until scoring. The programme comprised three generator families, 12 development cases and six held-out cases, with missingness, censoring, permutation controls and truth-blind prospective policy selection. Age coverage including abstention was 0.9655; specialist mean absolute error was 4.24 years versus 7.65 years for the competence-matched baseline. Reaction-family log loss was 0.896 versus 2.852, with coverage 0.739 and false commitment 0.038. Kinetic prediction passed with root-mean-square error 0.119 and interval coverage 1.0, but overall parameter identification was only 0.167 because the rate constant k and reactive surface area A were structurally confounded through k×A; parameter estimates were withheld when independent surface-area information was absent. The integrated policy passed its bounded cost-adjusted utility rule, with mean utility per cost 1.478 versus 0.246 for random, while a general-superiority claim remained withheld. No general topology-superiority gate was passed. Application to 320 Northern Ghana seasonal records, 41 Lower Anayari records and 63 Talensi records demonstrated data-readiness and chemistry-level diagnostics, not field validation of age, directed connectivity or unique reactions. HydroSheaf therefore supports a bounded synthetic-performance and auditable workflow claim, not universal groundwater-inference superiority.
