## Study design and datasets

### Ghanaian aquifer settings

Three real Ghanaian groundwater datasets were selected to span a deliberate range of hydrogeological setting and evidence completeness rather than to maximise sample size alone. The Northern Ghana dataset comprised 320 seasonal samples from 160 wells sampled in both the wet and dry seasons of March and August 2025, distributed across four administrative regions (Northern, North East, Upper East and Upper West) spanning the Voltaian sedimentary and Birimian/granitoid basement terrain typical of the area [@anku2008ghana; @banoengyakubo2011ghana]; no independent aquifer-type or lithology classification is available for these boreholes, so this study does not stratify Northern Ghana by lithological setting. The region shares the semi-arid-to-sub-humid seasonal rainfall regime typical of northern Ghana, in which a short, intense wet season and an extended dry season drive strong within-well seasonal concentration change, motivating the paired wet/dry sampling design used here. The Talensi dataset (63 samples) was drawn from an artisanal-small-scale-gold-mining-affected basement catchment in the Upper East Region, where silicate and carbonate weathering are reported as the main controls on groundwater chemistry alongside nitrate and trace-metal contamination linked to agrochemicals and wastewater [@chegbeleh2020talensi]. The Lower Anayari dataset (41 samples, referred to as "manu") was drawn from a shallow regolith/alluvial catchment in the same region where fluoride was among several factors, alongside sulphate, bicarbonate and nitrate, associated with reduced water-quality-index scores in a minority of samples; the great majority of samples in that catchment were classed as good quality, and the source study does not establish a geogenic origin for the fluoride specifically [@zakaria2021anayari]. All three settings are representative, in different ways, of the basement and regolith/alluvial hydrogeology that supplies most rural and peri-urban groundwater across northern Ghana, and none was selected because it was expected to give a particularly favourable or unfavourable result.

### Variable availability and evidence tiers

The three datasets differ systematically, not incidentally, in which diagnostic evidence they carry. Northern Ghana carries the fullest evidence stack used by this study: major ions, nitrate, fluoride, strontium, silica, and the stable isotopes delta18O and delta2H, all measured; calcite/dolomite/gypsum/halite/fluorite saturation indices are computed independently from this same measured chemistry via PHREEQC rather than supplied by the source workbook. No independent aquifer-type, geology-group, lithology, land-use, quality-control-class, or process-label metadata exists for these boreholes beyond region and district, and no directed graph of well connectivity is available; where the study reports a well-connectivity network, it is generated internally from chemical similarity, geographic proximity, or a randomised control, never imported. Northern Ghana was also sampled twice, in March (dry season) and August (wet season) 2025, giving a paired within-well seasonal design absent from the other two datasets. Lower Anayari carries major ions, nitrate, fluoride and the two stable isotopes, but no strontium, silica, or seasonal repeat sampling. Talensi carries major ions, nitrate, iron, redox potential and the two stable isotopes, but no fluoride, strontium, silica, seasonal repeat sampling or stated quality control. Both external datasets measure complete pH, temperature and major-ion panels, so the same independent PHREEQC saturation-index computation applied to Northern Ghana was also applied to them; saturation indices require only pH, temperature and major ions, not strontium or silica, so their absence from the tier ladder below reflects the ladder's own nested construction rather than a data ceiling, and they are used as an additional evidence-lift-gate lifter for both external datasets throughout (Methods). This structure was formalised as a five-level evidence-tier ladder, from Tier 0 (major ions only) to Tier 4 (majors, fluoride, isotopes, strontium/silica and saturation indices), against which each dataset's native ion-panel ceiling was fixed by what it actually contains: Tier 4 for Northern Ghana, Tier 2 for Lower Anayari, and Tier 1 for Talensi. The ladder is the experimental spine of the study: it is walked downward within Northern Ghana to quantify diagnostic value, and it is the reason the two external datasets cannot be treated as like-for-like replicates of Northern Ghana at a lower sample size ([[FIGREF:FIG-1]]).

### Why these datasets represent realistic data-limited conditions

These three datasets were treated as representative of, rather than exceptions to, the conditions under which African groundwater chemistry is typically interpreted: uneven tracer coverage across otherwise similar wells, single-campaign sampling for two of the three datasets, absent lithological and hydrogeological metadata for all three datasets at the individual-well level, and charge-balance quality that had to be recomputed independently rather than assumed, because no external quality-class label exists for any of the three datasets. An independent charge-balance audit, applied identically to all three datasets, confirmed this unevenness quantitatively and is reported as part of the Results.

A further boundary is definitional rather than empirical and is stated here because it governs every subsequent claim. An earlier stage of this study read a Northern Ghana workbook that additionally carried `Dominant_Process`, `Aquifer_Evolution_Label` and candidate `Graph_Edges` fields; those fields were themselves the output of a different, antecedent, non-independent analysis of the same boreholes, not measured or externally validated quantities, and reusing them would have imported that other study's own classifications and connectivity graph into what is meant to be an independent transfer test. They have therefore been removed from this study entirely rather than retained as a concordance reference: no claim in this study compares Hydrosheaf's inferred process labels or edge sets against them. Likewise, "Tier 4" denotes only the highest tier of the chemistry/isotope reaction-diagnostic ladder actually available in this study; it does not denote a complete, independently observed age-head-screen hydrogeological record, and no claim in this study should be read as asserting one.
