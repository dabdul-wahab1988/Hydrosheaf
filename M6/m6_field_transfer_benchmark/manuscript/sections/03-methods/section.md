## Hydrosheaf transfer workflow

### Datasets and harmonisation

Three real Ghanaian groundwater datasets were used: a Northern Ghana workbook of 320 seasonal samples from 160 wells (wet and dry campaigns, March and August 2025) spanning four administrative regions of the Voltaian sedimentary and Birimian/granitoid basement terrain typical of the area [@anku2008ghana; @banoengyakubo2011ghana]; an artisanal-mining-affected Talensi set of 63 samples [@chegbeleh2020talensi]; and a Lower Anayari (manu) set of 41 samples from a shallow regolith/alluvial catchment with documented geogenic fluoride occurrence [@zakaria2021anayari]. All ion concentrations were converted from mg/L to mmol/L by molar mass, isotope fields were read directly from the source worksheets, and Talensi longitudes were converted from a positive degrees-west convention to signed WGS84 values. No independent aquifer-type, geology-group or lithology classification exists for any of the three datasets at the individual-well level.

### Charge-balance screening and evidence tiers

An independent charge-balance error (CBE) was computed in meq/L from the harmonised ions and used to classify every sample as quantitative (|CBE| <=5%), screening (5-10%) or exploratory (>10%) [@fritz1994charge]. This classified Northern Ghana as 294 quantitative, 19 screening and 7 exploratory of 320 samples; primary analyses used the 294 quantitative records. No external quality-class label exists in the source data to check this classification against. A five-level evidence-tier ladder (Tier 0, major ions only, through Tier 4, majors plus fluoride, stable isotopes, strontium, silica and saturation indices) fixed the reaction-basis ion panel available to each dataset. Saturation indices for calcite, dolomite, gypsum, halite and fluorite were computed independently by live PHREEQC from each borehole or sample's own measured pH, temperature and major-ion chemistry, not supplied by any source workbook or file; because this computation needs only pH, temperature and major ions, it was applied identically to Northern Ghana, Talensi and Lower Anayari and used as an evidence-lift-gate lifter for all three, rather than withheld from the two external datasets on the assumption that it required strontium or silica. Native ion-panel tier ceilings were Tier 4 for Northern Ghana, Tier 2 for Lower Anayari and Tier 1 for Talensi, reflecting genuine absence of strontium, silica and seasonal repeat sampling from the two external datasets; saturation-index availability does not track this ion-panel ceiling and is reported separately.

### Inverse reaction inference

Each inference unit was a Cl-conservative-transport-corrected reactive residual: within-well wet-to-dry seasonal change for Northern Ghana, or the concentration difference along a candidate directed edge for the edge-based and external-transfer experiments. The conservative correction removed the concentration change attributable to evapoconcentration or dilution using chloride as a conservative tracer [@christophersen1992emma]:

<!-- EQ:EQ-1 -->
$$
r_i = x_{i}^{\text{target}} - f \, x_{i}^{\text{source}}, \qquad
f = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{x_{\text{Cl}}^{\text{target}}}{x_{\text{Cl}}^{\text{source}}},\, 0.2,\, 5.0\right)
$$

where $r_i$ is the reactive residual for ion $i$ and $f$ the estimated transport factor. The residual vector $\mathbf{r}$ was decomposed into non-negative, thermodynamically bounded reaction extents $\mathbf{z}$ using the frozen Milestone 5 (M5) sparse-regression estimator over a 16-reaction dictionary spanning silicate and carbonate weathering, cation exchange, evaporite dissolution, fluoride mobilisation, redox and nitrate loading [@tibshirani1996lasso; @zou2005elasticnet]:

<!-- EQ:EQ-2 -->
$$
\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{z} \,\in\, \mathcal{Z}}
\; \lVert \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z} - \mathbf{r} \rVert_2^2
+ \lambda_1 \lVert \mathbf{z} \rVert_1
+ \lambda_2 \lVert \mathbf{z} \rVert_2^2
$$

with $\mathbf{A}$ the stoichiometric reaction matrix restricted to the ions measured at the applicable evidence tier, $\lambda_1 = 0.01$, $\lambda_2 = 0.001$, and $\mathcal{Z}$ the non-negativity or saturation-index bound set; reactions requiring an unmeasured ion were penalised near-prohibitively so they could not be spuriously selected. Nothing in the M5 estimator or its calibration was re-fit on Ghanaian data: M6 is a transfer study.

Each inference unit was assigned one of four identifiability classes (non-identifiable, partially identifiable, equivalence-class, identifiable) and a 0-100 Mechanism Resolution Score (MRS) by projecting a nine-feature diagnostic vector (matrix-rank skill, coherence skill, bootstrap support stability, leave-one-ion held-out predictive skill, thermodynamic skill, and reconstruction statistics) through the frozen M5 calibration [@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto]. That calibration was fitted once, in the antecedent benchmark, on live-PHREEQC-generated synthetic scenarios spanning four hydrochemical archetypes with known reaction truth, and evaluated there on scenarios held out from fitting; it is applied here exactly as calibrated, with no parameter re-estimated on any Ghanaian sample, so that classification differences reported below reflect transfer to field data rather than local re-fitting. An evidence-lift gate then downgraded tracer-dependent process families (silicate, carbonate, evaporite, fluoride mobilisation) by one identifiability level whenever their corroborating tracer was absent or inconsistent (silica, strontium or saturation index for weathering; a deuterium-excess offset from the global meteoric water line for evaporite [@craig1961meteoric]; measured fluoride for fluoride mobilisation), so that a numerically confident fit could not be reported as resolved without independent chemical support. The fixed numeric thresholds defining tracer corroboration were predeclared operational thresholds rather than fitted to the Ghanaian data; they are not universal analytical reporting limits. A reviewer-requested low/default/high threshold analysis is reported separately from the locked primary analysis.

### Experiments, edges and robustness

[[FIG:FIG-2]]

Six experiments implementing the workflow summarised in [[FIGREF:FIG-2]] were executed on the harmonised data, in an order and with primary metrics fixed by a locked analysis plan before execution: dataset readiness and missingness; Northern Ghana seasonal-by-region transfer at Tier 4; a Tier 4-to-0 ablation quantifying identifiability loss as fluoride, isotopes and strontium/silica were withdrawn in turn; a flow-path-uncertainty experiment comparing three Hydrosheaf-generated candidate edge-construction strategies against each other (chemistry-similarity k-nearest-neighbours, geographic-nearest-neighbours, and a random/perturbed null set of matched size [@moracchini2025graphflow]); external transfer of the identical pipeline to Talensi and Lower Anayari at their native tiers, benchmarked against Northern Ghana re-run at matched tiers; and a limitation map contrasting conservative class-level reporting with single-best-fit reaction counts. Bootstrap resample counts for the support-stability diagnostic ranged from 8 to 48 depending on experiment, set for runtime rather than precision reasons; the count used for each reported statistic is stated in Supplementary Methods Section S4 and should be considered when comparing stability values across tables. Supporting robustness analyses (Supplementary Methods) tested whether the evidence gate alone drove apparent identifiability loss, validated the tier-collapse direction against synthetic data with known reaction truth, tested sensitivity to the transport-correction choice, diagnosed the Talensi charge imbalance, and screened candidate edges for competing no-flow explanations. A within-campaign one-step seasonal hold-forward benchmark, reused unmodified from the Milestone 7.2 (M7.2) supporting validation, compared persistence, an expanding mean-delta baseline and a HydroSheaf graph-ridge predictor.

### Reproducibility

The workflow was executed deterministically (fixed seed) via a single orchestrating script, with all derived tables and Nature-style figures generated in one reproducible run. Full derivations, algorithmic detail and secondary validation, sensitivity and robustness protocols are reported in the Supplementary Methods. An earlier stage of this study read a Northern Ghana workbook that additionally carried inferred `Dominant_Process`, `Aquifer_Evolution_Label` and `Graph_Edges` fields from a different, antecedent, non-independent analysis of the same boreholes; these have been removed entirely rather than retained as a concordance reference, so no claim in this study compares Hydrosheaf's inferred process labels or edge sets against them [@beven2001glue].

The frozen M5 inverse solver, MRS calibration and M7.2 hold-forward outputs on which this workflow depends are components of the same research programme as this study and are not, at the time of writing, independently peer-reviewed references. Analysis code, derived outputs, and access notes are maintained at `https://github.com/dabdul-wahab1988/Hydrosheaf`. The submitted version must also state the access conditions and source citations for the third-party raw measurements.
