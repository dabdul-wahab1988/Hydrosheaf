## Discussion

### What Hydrosheaf adds beyond conventional chemistry interpretation

Conventional hydrochemical interpretation of these three settings would typically proceed from Gibbs and Piper-type diagrams and facies classification to a single narrative account of dominant process [@gibbs1970mechanisms; @piper1944graphic] (Supplementary Fig. S1). That approach would have reported a broadly similar headline story for Northern Ghana, silicate weathering dominant across the sampled region, with cation exchange, redox and nitrate loading as secondary processes, because the underlying chemistry is genuinely dominated by these processes.

What it would not have reported is the tier ablation's central finding: that a numerically confident, well-fitting reaction estimate persisted essentially unchanged in apparent quality as strontium and silica were withdrawn, even though the fraction of wells for which silicate weathering could be independently corroborated collapsed from effectively complete to just under half. That collapse is itself the output of a designed conservatism prior, the evidence-lift gate, rather than a direct empirical measurement; its direction, but not its exact numerical magnitude, was independently validated against synthetic data with known truth. A workflow that reports goodness-of-fit alongside an explicit, tracer-conditioned identifiability class, and that discloses the gate as a conservatism prior rather than an intrinsic property of the data, makes this gap visible; one that reports fit quality alone does not. That is the specific, demonstrated value added here, and it is a value that shows up only once the workflow is stress-tested against withdrawn evidence rather than evaluated on the best-available dataset alone.

### Where it reduces overinterpretation and where it cannot resolve equifinality

The synthetic ground-truth validation showed that exact-mineral identification remained limited (F1 approximately 0.5-0.7) even at the richest evidence tier, while family- and class-level recovery was substantially better and improved specifically where added tracers were mechanistically relevant. This is consistent with the broader equifinality literature's observation that non-uniqueness in reaction-path and mass-balance inference is a structural property of the problem, not a defect specific to this workflow or this dataset [@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto]. Reporting at the class or family level, and admitting on average 7.1 alternative reactions where a single best fit would commit to 5.4, is therefore not a hedge but a calibrated response to a genuine, quantified limit: the exact reaction cannot be identified from this evidence, in this system, regardless of which inference method is used.

Where the workflow adds discipline is in making that limit explicit and dataset-specific rather than leaving it implicit in an unstated single answer. It cannot, and does not claim to, resolve the underlying non-uniqueness; the edge-uncertainty experiment showed the same pattern at the network level, where the assumed connectivity changed which process network was inferred without changing whether any individual point-to-point inference was itself resolvable.

### Relationship to the antecedent Northern Ghana field application

The antecedent synthetic benchmark from which the inverse solver and Mechanism Resolution Score calibration are frozen also included its own chemistry-only field application to Northern Ghana wet/dry well pairs, and the population analysed there overlaps substantially with the 160-well Northern Ghana dataset used for the seasonal transfer experiment reported here. This overlap is stated explicitly rather than left implicit. What this study adds beyond that prior application is the full Tier 4 evidence stack (isotopes, fluoride, strontium/silica, and saturation indices computed independently by PHREEQC from the same measured chemistry) rather than majors alone, the region-stratified breakdown, and, more substantially, the four additional experiments that the prior application did not include: the tier-by-tier ablation that produces the paper's central fit-quality-versus-identifiability finding, the edge-construction sensitivity analysis, transfer to two external datasets, and the synthesised limitation map. The seasonal-transfer experiment (E2) on its own, considered in isolation from these four additional experiments, would not constitute a sufficient independent contribution, and it is not presented as one; it functions here as the Tier 4 reference point against which the tier-ablation, edge-uncertainty and external-transfer experiments are all benchmarked.

### Monitoring implications: the next-best measurement per dataset

Because the tier-ablation and external-transfer results isolate strontium and silica as the specific measurements whose absence drives Lower Anayari's near-total non-identifiability and a substantial share of Northern Ghana's tier-3-to-tier-2 collapse, the practical implication is concrete rather than generic: for a silicate-dominated, data-limited Ghanaian aquifer, adding strontium and silica to an otherwise standard major-ion-plus-isotope panel is a testable candidate for reducing ambiguity. The present comparison does not establish that adding these tracers is superior to adding wells, because panel completeness and dataset sample size are confounded in the external-transfer analysis. Strontium and silica have long been used as discriminants between silicate- and carbonate-derived solutes in hydrogeochemistry; the contribution here is not the discovery that these tracers matter, which is established domain knowledge, but a dataset-specific, quantified demonstration of how much identifiability is lost when they are absent and a transferable illustration of how the same tier-ablation logic could be applied to price the value of any other candidate measurement. Fluoride mobilisation, a documented regional concern given widespread geogenic fluoride occurrence in Ghanaian and wider West African groundwater [@podgorski2022fluoride; @onipe2020fluoride], and agro-domestic nitrate loading [@tredoux2006nitrate] both remained majors-corroborable and comparatively stable across tiers in this study, so the measurement-value argument here is specific to weathering-family resolution and should not be generalised to every process class without re-running the same tier-ablation logic for the process of interest.

### Interpretation and honesty boundary of the field application

This study evaluated field transfer of a chemistry-based reaction-inference audit; it did not, and could not, independently validate groundwater residence time, exact directed flow paths, or the true reaction mechanism at any well, consistent with the boundary stated in Study design. An earlier stage of this study compared Hydrosheaf's inferred process labels against a different, antecedent study's own prior process labels for the same boreholes; because those labels are themselves the non-independent output of another analysis rather than measured or externally validated quantities, that comparison has been removed rather than reported, and no claim here rests on agreement or disagreement with them. The within-campaign seasonal hold-forward result likewise supports a narrow claim about chemistry predictability and says nothing about residence time or topology. The appropriate reading of these results is that an identifiability-aware workflow can transfer usefully to real, uneven African aquifer data as a conservative audit of what current evidence supports, while remaining explicit that it is not, and does not claim to be, a substitute for independent hydraulic, isotopic-age or tracer-based validation of flow and residence time.

Five limits follow directly and bound the scope of any generalisation from these results. First, only one of the three datasets reached the tier at which strontium/silica-based corroboration was possible, so the tier-ablation result, while internally validated against known synthetic truth, has been demonstrated as a within-Ghana transfer and not yet as a transfer across substantially different lithological provinces elsewhere in West Africa. Second, the within-campaign hold-forward benchmark evaluated one dry season following one wet season at one set of wells; it does not establish predictive skill across multiple years or across a genuine change in climatic or abstraction regime. Third, every robustness and validation analysis reported here was performed on the same three datasets used for the headline results, so the next natural test of this workflow is prospective application to an independently collected dataset with strontium, silica and repeat seasonal sampling by design, which would allow the next-best-measurement recommendation itself to be evaluated rather than only motivated.

Fourth, the numeric thresholds defining tracer corroboration in the evidence-lift gate are operational choices rather than universal analytical reporting limits. The threshold-profile sensitivity analysis showed only a narrow change in Tier 2 non-identifiability (51.3-53.1%) and Tier 3 non-identifiability (0.0-2.5%) across the tested profiles, but the precise collapse magnitude remains conditional on this tested range. Fifth, the external-transfer comparison assumes that chemistry-similarity edge networks built within Talensi's 63 and Lower Anayari's 41 samples are structurally comparable to the Northern Ghana reference network built within a much larger pool of 320 samples; nearest-neighbour graphs constructed within smaller pools are likely to differ structurally from those built within larger ones independent of any tracer-availability effect, and this sample-size-dependent confound has not been separated from the tier effect this study set out to isolate.
