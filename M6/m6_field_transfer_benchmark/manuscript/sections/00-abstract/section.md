Groundwater process interpretation across semi-arid West Africa often relies on uneven field evidence. We tested whether a previously benchmarked, identifiability-aware sparse inverse reaction workflow, with calibration fixed, transfers to one well-resourced and two sparse Ghanaian groundwater datasets on a five-level evidence-tier ladder. At full evidence, all 160 Northern Ghana wells were conservatively classed as partially identifiable. Removing strontium and silica sent 52% to non-identifiable under a predeclared operational evidence gate, while fit quality barely changed. Lower Anayari, which lacks those tracers, returned 95.3% non-identifiable candidate edges. Talensi failed charge-balance screening and is therefore reported only as an exploratory screening case. Flow-path assumptions changed the inferred process network but not point identifiability. Wet-to-dry chemistry was predictable beyond no change, but not clearly beyond a simple seasonal baseline. The framework identifies which interpretations survive sparse evidence and where an additional measurement may reduce ambiguity; it does not validate true reactions, flow paths, or ages.
