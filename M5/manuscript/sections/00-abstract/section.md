# Abstract

Inverse geochemical models can fit observed concentration changes without
uniquely identifying the responsible reactions, a non-uniqueness problem
that undermines single best-fit groundwater interpretations. The aim was to
develop and validate, within Hydrosheaf, a groundwater-process framework, an
identifiability-aware sparse inversion approach integrating
reaction-equivalence classes, a Mechanism Resolution Score, held-out-ion
falsification, and next-best-measurement selection, in a controlled
benchmark and a chemistry-only field transfer. The bounded guarded model was
benchmarked on 240 live-PHREEQC scenarios across four
hydrochemical archetypes (21,600 factorial inverse fits) and applied to 160
same-borehole wet/dry pairs from Northern Ghana. Results showed that, in the
synthetic benchmark, 55% of the lowest-residual quartile of fits still had
exact-phase F1 below 0.80. The guarded model achieved
mean exact-phase and equivalence-class F1 of 0.563 and 0.609 with a
false-discovery rate of 0.361, improving on an otherwise identical
thermodynamic elastic-net comparator (0.465).
Thermodynamic screening removed impossible reactions but left 72.5% (174/240;
95% Wilson interval 66.5%-77.8%) of the predeclared 3%-noise, full-panel
constrained fits below equivalence-class F1 0.80; a conventional PHREEQC
baseline generated 185.8 feasible models per scenario, and the
resolution score reached 48.9% accuracy on an untouched archetype, versus
37.6% for majority-class prediction. The
significance is that the approach prevents overinterpretation of
non-identifiable pathways, replacing single best-fit narratives with a
measurable resolution hierarchy. The Ghana component demonstrates
computability on incomplete field chemistry; it is not independent validation
of reaction mechanisms, flow paths, or ages.
