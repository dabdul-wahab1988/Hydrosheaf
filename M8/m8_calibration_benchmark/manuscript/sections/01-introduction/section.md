## Introduction

Groundwater models are routinely calibrated against head, concentration and
tracer observations, and a converged calibration with a small objective
function is often treated as evidence that the model has been identified.
That inference is invalid in general. Equifinality research has established
that many different parameter sets can reproduce the same observations
adequately, so a good fit does not select a unique model or parameter vector
[@beven2001glue; @beven2006manifesto]. Uncertainty about model predictions
therefore cannot be read off the calibration residual; it must be propagated
explicitly from parameters and structure [@neuman2003bma]. In groundwater
inverse problems the same dissociation appears systematically: the optimiser
can converge, the objective can be small, and the recovered parameters can
still be wrong. Yet the standard reporting practice in applied calibration
studies emphasises convergence diagnostics and residual statistics, with
identifiability treated as a secondary or implicit concern.

The broader HydroSheaf programme has repeatedly hit this limit. In the M3-M7
benchmarks, inference targets were validated against another model's output
(USGS/TracerLPM ages, MODPATH particle tracks), so the comparison ceiling was
emulation rather than ground truth. Synthetic calibration removes that
ceiling: parameters are known exactly, and recovery can be measured directly.
M8 therefore uses controlled synthetic experiments in which the truth is
known by construction, while treating the domain adapters, optimiser and
diagnostic pipeline as the production software under test.

This paper addresses three questions. First, how well does the production
inverse engine recover known parameters, and how well are its linearised
uncertainty intervals calibrated, across sampling designs that are
identifiable, correlated or over-parameterised by construction? Second, do
Fisher-information criteria for next-measurement selection transfer from
predicted information to realised recovery, and does the recommended design
remain useful when truth is generated by an independent numerical solver
rather than the same analytical model used for calibration? The transfer of
optimal monitoring designs to field conditions is itself a recognised open
problem in groundwater modelling [@sreekanth2017monitoring]. Third, can the
existing categorical active-learning module make an actionable measurement
recommendation, and does a prospectively specified Bayesian
value-of-information workflow improve sequential measurement design over
random acquisition while remaining noninferior to a strong legacy policy?

The motivation also carries over from the tracer-age and topology strands of
the programme: environmental-tracer inference is limited by model and mixing
assumptions [@mccallum2015limitations; @visser2013multitracer], and
graph-based transport approximation is itself an active research area
[@moracchini2025graphflow; @linde2006jointinversion]. In each of those strands
the comparison target was another model's output, so the ceiling was emulation
rather than accuracy. Data-limited aquifer settings further constrain what any
calibration can claim [@macdonald2012africa]. Controlled known-truth
calibration is therefore the common thread that closes the audit loop across
the programme.

The contribution is the measured dissociation between optimisation success,
objective value and parameter identifiability, and the diagnostic suite that
makes it visible - not the optimiser itself, which is a standard
Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt engine. The scope is explicitly controlled
synthetic evaluation through production HydroSheaf interfaces; no field
validation claim is made anywhere in this paper.

### Claims and exclusions

The M8 pilot exploration identified five candidate claims (C1: optimiser
success, objective value and identifiability come apart; C2: nominal 95%
intervals undercover in identifiable designs at realistic noise; C3: the
covariance fallback detects exact rank deficiency only, and the condition
number is the better flag; C3b: a transferable error threshold near
cond(FIM) ~ 10^2-10^3; C3c: coverage rises to 1.00 as problems become
unidentifiable because intervals inflate). These were generated in
exploratory pilot runs that were inspected before any prospective protocol
was locked, so they are excluded from the confirmatory contribution of this
paper. They remain hypothesis-generating diagnostics and are not presented
as confirmed results.

The confirmatory contribution consists of the locked runs described in
Section 2: a matched-model transport optimal-design benchmark
(RUN-M8-CONFIRM-20260728-01), an independent-model robustness benchmark
(RUN-M8-INDEPENDENT-20260728-01), and a prospectively qualified topology
active-learning benchmark (RUN-M8-FRONTIER-AL-20260728-01). Every claim
below is scoped to controlled synthetic evaluation, and negative and
undemonstrated results are stated alongside positive ones.
