# Peer review report

**Manuscript:** HydroSheaf benchmarks: a harmonized identifiability comparison of directed connectivity, residence-time, and reaction inference in data-limited aquifers
**Target venue:** Computers & Geosciences (research article)
**Files reviewed:** `O3/manuscript/Manuscript-O3.md`, `O3/manuscript/Manuscript-O3.docx`, supporting materials in `O3/DECISIONS.md`, `O3/Outline.md`, `O3/proposal.normalized.json`, `O3/analysis/`
**Reviewer note on scope:** This is a benchmark-synthesis article built entirely on already-locked result files from three sister projects (`M3`, `M4`, `M5`), not a primary data-collection study. Several standard criteria (sampling strategy, IRB/ethics, RCT reporting standards) do not apply and are marked as such below.

---

## 1. Title and Abstract

1. **[Minor]** The title is accurate but front-loads "HydroSheaf benchmarks," a project-internal brand name, ahead of the actual scientific content. A reader scanning Computers & Geosciences' table of contents has no way to know from the title alone that this is a cross-domain identifiability comparison covering three unrelated physical processes (advective transport, radiometric/tracer decay, and aqueous geochemistry). Retitle to foreground the comparison itself, for example "Recall exceeds precision under independent evaluation: a harmonized benchmark comparison across connectivity, age, and reaction inference in groundwater systems," and demote "HydroSheaf" to a parenthetical or subtitle.

2. **[Major]** The abstract states the paper's contribution is a "shared vocabulary and a computational-cost accounting that none of the three underlying benchmarks reports alone," but this framing undersells what the paper actually claims later, which is a substantive empirical pattern (capture exceeds correctness in all three layers). An abstract that describes its own contribution primarily as bookkeeping ("vocabulary," "accounting") will read to a reviewer as low-stakes and will invite exactly the "salami slicing" and "insufficient contribution" objections raised in Sections 8 and the Research Integrity scan below. Rewrite the closing sentence to state the empirical finding as the primary contribution and the taxonomy/accounting as supporting infrastructure, not the other way around.

3. **[Major]** The abstract reports point estimates only (recall 0.845, precision 0.487, and so on) with no uncertainty. The source table for the reaction layer, `table1_comparative_inverse_performance.csv`, carries 95% confidence intervals for every one of these quantities (for example, Hydrosheaf Guarded phase F1 is reported there as "0.563 [0.525, 0.602]"), and the topology layer's edge count (174 reference edges) is small enough that a precision of 0.487 carries real sampling uncertainty. Reporting bare point estimates in the abstract of a paper whose entire argument rests on comparing the relative size of two numbers per layer overstates the precision of the comparison. Add at least the reaction-layer confidence intervals to the abstract and carry interval estimates through to Table 2 and Figure 2 (see Issue 3 under Methodology).

4. **[Minor]** The abstract's closing sentence, "it is offered as a companion to the HydroSheaf framework article, not a fourth validation of any component," is a defensive hedge that would be more persuasive placed in the Discussion (where it already appears, Section 5.5) than in the abstract, where it reads as the authors pre-emptively arguing with a reviewer who has not yet objected to anything. Abstracts should state what the paper does, not what it is not. Move this framing sentence out of the abstract and use the freed word count for the confidence-interval addition in Issue 3.

## 2. Introduction

1. **[Major]** The Introduction never engages with the existing literature on multi-model or multi-method benchmark comparison in hydrology and hydrogeology. There is a substantial body of work on model intercomparison, cross-validation of parameter-estimation methods (for example, the PEST and DREAM ecosystems), and ensemble/multi-model evaluation frameworks that asks precisely the kind of question this paper asks: how do heterogeneous inference methods compare when judged against a common standard? The manuscript's own related-work paragraph (Section 1, paragraph 2) cites only Prediction in Ungauged Basins and GLUE, both about within-model equifinality, not about cross-method benchmark harmonization. A reviewer working in computational hydrogeology will ask why this paper does not cite or position itself against any prior benchmark-harmonization or model-intercomparison study. Add a paragraph identifying at least two or three prior multi-method or multi-model comparison frameworks in groundwater or environmental modelling, and state explicitly what is different about harmonizing three physically distinct inference problems (as opposed to comparing several models of the same problem).

2. **[Major]** The stated central argument, that "recall/sensitivity substantially exceeds precision/specificity" in all three layers, is presented in the Introduction as though it is a single, unified empirical regularity. But the paper's own Methods (Section 3.4) admits the three "recall" and "precision" analogues are not the same statistic and are not even computed the same way (macro-averaged per-scenario means for two of the three, a micro-averaged pooled count for the third). The Introduction should flag this limitation before making the claim, not only in the Methods forty paragraphs later. As written, a reader who stops after the Introduction (which is how many readers use introductions) will come away believing this is a clean, quantitatively unified finding. Add one sentence in the Introduction's contribution paragraph explicitly noting that the three metrics are analogous, not identical, so the claim's actual epistemic status is visible from the first read.

3. **[Minor]** The Introduction states that HydroSheaf's three inference layers were "designed, executed and locked independently, on its own timetable, against its own external reference," which is offered as evidence of the pattern's generality. But all three were built by the same author, under the same broader project philosophy (evidence-gating, abstention, claim ledgers), documented in the same style of `DECISIONS.md` file. This is not independence in the sense a reader would assume from the word; it is closer to three applications of one methodological doctrine. State this plainly rather than letting "independently" carry more weight than it can bear, and rely on the more accurate and already-present hedge in Discussion Section 5.3 ("cannot rule out a shared design habit") earlier, in the Introduction, not just at the end.

4. **[Minor]** The gap statement, "no single HydroSheaf component paper closes" this comparison gap, is true but narrow. The Introduction would be stronger if it argued the gap exists in the field generally (no groundwater study anywhere has harmonized connectivity, age, and reaction benchmarks under one taxonomy), rather than only within this one research programme's own paper set. As written, the gap is framed almost entirely in terms of the authors' own prior work, which weakens the case that this is a contribution to the field rather than internal bookkeeping. Broaden the gap statement with at least one sentence about the absence of this kind of harmonization in the wider literature, supported by the citation added per Issue 1.

## 3. Methodology

1. **[Critical]** The paper's central comparative device, plotting a "capture-type" axis against a "correctness-type" axis for three layers that use three different statistics computed three different ways, is disclosed as a "methodological choice" (Section 3.4) but is never validated as a sound way to support a scientific claim. Topology's precision and recall are a genuine, textbook confusion-matrix pair over 174 labelled edges. Reaction's pair mixes a macro-averaged per-scenario precision (M5's own published number) with a micro-averaged pooled recall the authors computed themselves for this paper (Section 3.4, Table 2 notes) because no equivalent macro-averaged recall exists. Age's pair is, by the authors' own admission, "not a literal recall/precision pair" at all, but a reportability rate compared against a within-factor-2 agreement rate, two quantities with no formal relationship to each other. Building a paper's headline figure and central claim on three quantities that are acknowledged not to be commensurable is a methodological problem that disclosure alone does not fix; a reader has no principled way to know whether the "0.358 gap" reported for topology and the "0.040 gap" reported for reaction (Section 4.2) mean anything relative to each other, because the two gaps are differences between different kinds of things. This must be addressed by either (a) restricting the cross-component comparison to a genuinely shared, formally defined statistic, accepting that this will narrow the scope of the claim, or (b) explicitly and quantitatively characterizing the incommensurability (for example, reporting what range of values each metric type can even take, and under what conditions the analogy breaks down) rather than a single disclosure sentence. As submitted, this is fixable through revision, so it does not by itself trigger the Critical Flaw Override, but it must be resolved before the central claim can be evaluated as a genuine empirical finding rather than a rhetorical framing.

2. **[Major]** Section 3.4 states that the reaction-layer recall value (0.599) is "not identical to any single already-published headline number" and was constructed for this paper by pooling per-reaction confusion counts from `M5`'s own supplementary table. `O3/DECISIONS.md` Decision D2 states plainly that "O3 performs no re-simulation" and that every number is either read directly or is "a harmonization-layer statistic... computed by `O3/analysis/python/` directly from those files with no change to the underlying inference." Pooling confusion counts across sixteen reactions to produce a number that appears nowhere in the source project is a new statistic, not a harmonization of an existing one, even though it is computed from already-published counts and even though the arithmetic is disclosed. The paper needs to either justify why this counts as harmonization rather than new analysis under its own stated rule, or relabel it honestly as a new derived statistic and discuss what assumptions justify pooling across sixteen chemically different reactions (which are not exchangeable; some reactions are far easier to detect than others, as the paper's own Figure 2 discussion of the reaction-layer noise-tier and ion-panel results implies).

3. **[Major]** No confidence intervals, standard errors, or resampling-based uncertainty are carried into the paper's own comparisons, despite being available in the source data for at least the reaction layer (`table1_comparative_inverse_performance.csv` reports 95% CIs for every metric used in this paper) and computable in principle for the topology layer from its 174-edge confusion matrix (a standard binomial or bootstrap interval on precision and recall would take one line of R). Table 2 and Table 3 report bare point estimates. Without uncertainty bounds, a reader cannot tell whether topology's 0.845-versus-0.487 gap and reaction's 0.599-versus-0.639 near-parity are both statistically distinguishable patterns or whether one of them is within noise. Given that Section 4.2 explicitly argues the gap "differs across layers" and assigns numeric magnitudes to that difference (0.358, 0.305, 0.040), the absence of uncertainty on quantities being compared to the third decimal place is a real gap. Add confidence intervals (from the source CIs where available, bootstrap from raw edge/reaction counts otherwise) to Table 2, Table 3, and Figures 2 to 3, and qualify the "magnitude of the gap differs" claim in Section 4.2 accordingly.

4. **[Major]** Section 3.1 states that residual staleness risk from the 2026-08-01/02 `hydrosheaf/validation` code changes "is disclosed in Limitations rather than assumed away," and Discussion Section 5.6 does disclose it, but the check performed is a code-reading exercise, not an empirical one: the authors read which modules the recent commits touched and reasoned that `M3` and `M4`'s core inference paths were unaffected. No regression test, hash comparison, or re-run of even a small subset of either benchmark under current code was performed to confirm this reasoning empirically. For a paper whose only methodological contribution is trusting that three separately timed result sets remain valid and comparable, a code-reading argument is weaker evidence than it is presented as. At minimum, re-run a small, fast subset of `M3`'s or `M4`'s benchmark (for example, the Head-gradient topology scenario, which is inexpensive) under current code and confirm the headline number is unchanged, and report that confirmation rather than only the module-dependency argument.

5. **[Minor]** The Methods section describes the taxonomy categories (independent, non-independent, informed control, negative control, sensitivity diagnostic, prior informed, conventional baseline) but does not state who assigned each of the 25 rows to its category, whether any inter-rater or consistency check was performed, or whether any row was ambiguous between two categories. Given that the taxonomy is the paper's first stated contribution (Objective 1), its construction deserves the same methodological transparency as any other classification scheme. State explicitly that categorization was performed by the sole author against each source component's own claim ledger, and note any borderline cases (for example, whether the age layer's "hierarchical old-water prior," which `M3`'s own decisions record as withdrawn for non-independence reasons, was considered for inclusion here and why it was excluded).

## 4. Results and Discussion

1. **[Major]** Section 4.2 reports that "the magnitude of the gap differs across layers" and ranks topology as showing "the largest separation," immediately followed by the caveat that age's two axes "are not the same kind of rate as the other two." This ordering, a quantitative ranking followed by a footnote undermining the comparability of two of the three items being ranked, buries the more important point. A reader skimming for the headline result will retain "topology shows the largest separation" and not the caveat that makes that ranking only two-thirds valid. Restructure this paragraph so the incommensurability caveat comes first, and drop or heavily qualify the ranking language ("largest separation") given that only two of the three quantities being ranked (topology and reaction, both true precision/recall pairs) are directly comparable to each other in the first place.

2. **[Major]** Discussion Section 5.3 argues that the same pattern appearing across three benchmarks is "weak evidence that the signature reflects something general about data-limited groundwater inference," immediately followed by an honest acknowledgment that "a comparison of three benchmarks from one research programme cannot rule out a shared design habit as the explanation." This is a genuinely well-hedged sentence, but the paper does not do anything to distinguish between the two explanations it raises. A stronger paper would attempt at least one falsification move, for example checking whether any published, independently authored groundwater benchmark (not part of this project) shows the same recall-over-precision asymmetry, which would support the "general phenomenon" reading, or would show the pattern is specific to how this project's benchmarks were designed (all three use a broadly similar evidence-gating philosophy), which would support the "shared design habit" reading. As written, the paper raises the right question and then declines to investigate it. Either add a brief comparison against one externally published, non-HydroSheaf groundwater inference benchmark that reports a comparable statistic, or reframe the claim more modestly as a within-project observation without the "something general" language.

3. **[Major]** The reaction-layer "Mechanism Resolution Score" result (Section 4.3) is described as "a genuine transfer test rather than a same-fold emulation," reaching "48.9% four-class accuracy against a 25.0% chance level." The paper treats this four-fold improvement over chance as informative, but a four-class classifier that is right about half the time is, by ordinary standards in machine learning benchmark reporting, a weak classifier, and the paper does not report any measure of per-class performance (for example, is 48.9% driven by good performance on one easy class and near-chance performance on the other three, which a confusion matrix would reveal). Given that the paper elsewhere insists on precision about what a number does and does not show, treating "reaches double chance level" as validating the tool without a class-level breakdown is inconsistent with the paper's own stated standard of rigor. Report the four-class confusion matrix for the held-out mixed archetype, or explicitly note this is not reported and flag per-class performance as an open question.

4. **[Minor]** Section 4.4 reports that the "non-independent age-fraction scenario... shows the highest within-factor-2 agreement of the four design-matrix scenarios plotted (0.917), above every independent scenario including strict parity (0.875)." This is presented as a neutral data point, but it is actually a meaningful negative result for the paper's own broader argument: a scenario that leaks reference information into the fit outperforms every independent scenario, which is exactly the kind of pattern the paper's abstention-and-guarding philosophy is designed to catch and flag, yet the paper does not connect this observation back to its own central thesis anywhere in the Discussion. Add a sentence in Discussion Section 5.1 or 5.2 noting that this specific result is a small-scale demonstration of the same non-independence-inflates-apparent-performance pattern that Section 5.2 discusses at the layer level, which would strengthen rather than weaken the paper's overall argument.

5. **[Minor]** The Limitations section (5.6) lists five limitations but omits any discussion of what would happen to the paper's central claim if the taxonomy in Table 1 were constructed differently, for example if a different informed reviewer disagreed with two or three of the 25 row classifications. Given that the taxonomy directly determines which rows are compared as "independent" in Section 4.2's headline claim, some sensitivity discussion (even qualitative: "reclassifying the Hodge-pruned and projected-gradient rows, which are diagnostic variants rather than independent scenarios in their own right, would not change the headline topology numbers because all three collapse to the same F1") would strengthen confidence that the central result is not an artifact of one classification choice.

## 5. Tables and Figures

1. **[Major]** Figure 4 (within-component detail) plots each component's retained rows on "each component's own headline metric," but the three panels use three different metrics on three visually similar bar-chart axes without a shared caption note reminding the reader that the y-axis means something different in each panel. A reader who has not just finished Section 3.4 could easily compare bar heights across the three panels of Figure 4 as if they were on the same scale, which the paper's own Methods explicitly says they are not. Add an explicit per-panel axis label or a caption sentence stating that the three panels are not on a comparable scale, matching the discipline already applied in Figure 3's caption.

2. **[Minor]** Table 1 collapses the Hodge-pruned and projected-gradient topology rows into the taxonomy with a "(diagnostic)" tag but does not explain in the table itself why these are retained as separate rows when they are numerically identical to the Head-gradient row (F1 0.618 in all three cases, per the table). A reader encountering Table 1 without having read Methods Section 3.2 in detail would reasonably wonder whether this is three independent pieces of evidence or one result reported three times. Add a one-line table note stating that these three rows are diagnostic variants of the same underlying scenario and are not independent replications.

3. **[Minor]** None of the six figures reports sample size or n directly on the plot (it appears only in table notes or body text). For a paper whose whole argument depends on comparing metrics computed from very different amounts of underlying data (174 topology edges versus 21,600 reaction fits versus 1,272 age candidate rows), putting n directly on each bar or panel, not only in a separate table, would let a reader assess the reliability of each bar at a glance without cross-referencing text. Add n labels to Figures 2, 3, and 4.

4. **[Minor]** Table 4's caption describes "recorded runtime" as a benchmark-scale comparator, but the resulting claim in the text and Figure 5, that "only the reaction benchmark records per-fit runtime," is presented as though it reflects a meaningful design choice by the age and topology benchmark authors, when it more likely reflects that those two benchmarks were not built with computational-cost reporting as a goal at all (nothing in `M3` or `M4`'s own manifests suggests runtime was ever a consideration). Describing an absence of a design goal as a comparative "finding" overstates what Table 4 shows. Soften the framing to state plainly that runtime was not a design goal for two of the three benchmarks, rather than implying a like-for-like comparison was attempted and one benchmark simply did not report it.

## 6. References

1. **[Major]** The three sister manuscripts this paper depends on for every reported number, `M3`, `M4`, and `M5`, are referenced throughout the text using inline code-style backticks (`` `M3` ``, `` `M4` ``, `` `M5` ``) rather than as formal citations, and none of them appears in the bibliography. A reader of the published paper has no way to locate, request, or verify any of the underlying result files this paper's entire argument rests on, because they are neither cited nor (at submission) publicly deposited (see Data Availability, below). At minimum, add formal citations for `M2.3`, `M3`, `M4`, and `M5` as "manuscript in preparation" or "data set" entries with author, working title, and target venue, so a reader has a citable handle on the source material even before those companion works are public.

2. **[Minor]** The reference list cites Beven and Freer's GLUE paper and Beven's equifinality manifesto but does not cite any more recent (post-2010) critique or extension of the equifinality/GLUE framework, several of which exist in the hydrological literature and would let the Discussion's equifinality framing (Section 5.3) engage with how the concept has evolved rather than resting entirely on two papers from 2001 and 2006. Add at least one more recent citation discussing equifinality or structural uncertainty in hydrological or hydrogeological model evaluation.

3. **[Minor]** Citation spot-check (performed via web search on three references): Beven and Freer (2001, Journal of Hydrology 249:11-29), Pollock (2016, USGS OFR 2016-1086), and Oreskes, Shrader-Frechette, and Belitz (1994, Science 263:641-646) were all verified to exist, to match the bibliographic details given (title, journal, volume, pages, year), and to be used in a manner consistent with what each source actually argues. No fabrication or misrepresentation was found in this spot-check. This is a genuine strength of the bibliography and should be maintained as new citations are added per Issues 1 and 2.

## 7. Editorial and Language Quality

1. **[Minor]** The manuscript is, on the whole, clearly written, with consistent terminology (the taxonomy vocabulary from Methods, "capture-type" and "correctness-type," is used identically in Results and Discussion without drift) and a coherent argumentative thread from Introduction through Conclusion. This is worth stating plainly rather than only noting problems.

2. **[Minor]** A small number of sentences run long enough to require re-reading, for example the Section 5.1 sentence beginning "This is not a claim that the three numbers are statistically identical, nor that one underlying cause explains all three... [continuing for three more clauses]." Breaking sentences like this into two shorter sentences would improve readability without losing content. Example: split into "This is not a claim that the three numbers are statistically identical. It is also not a claim that one underlying cause explains all three; the paper takes no position on whether a shared cause exists."

3. **[Minor]** The manuscript's heavy reliance on em-dash-style double-hyphen constructions ("--") throughout (dozens of instances across Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion) creates a slightly breathless, clause-stacking rhythm across the whole paper. Varying sentence construction so that not every qualifying clause is set off with a dash would improve pacing, particularly in Discussion Section 5.2, which uses this construction four times in five sentences.

4. **[Minor]** Numeral formatting is mostly consistent (values under one reported to three decimal places, percentages to one decimal place) but there are occasional lapses, for example "log10 R2 0.482" in some places and "log10 R2 of 0.482" in others, and "F1 0.618" versus "F1 of 0.618." This is a minor copy-editing pass, not a substantive issue, but should be regularized before submission.

## 8. Novelty, Contribution, and Significance

1. **[Major]** The paper's stated contribution, in its own words (Discussion Section 5.5), is "the comparison itself: the taxonomy... the disclosed reconciliation of their different averaging conventions... the computational-scale accounting... and the explicit statement of where field- and archive-transfer evidence exists." Every one of these four things is a synthesis or bookkeeping exercise over material the authors already produced and, in three of four cases (`M3`, `M4`, `M5`), already reported individually. None of the four is a new experiment, a new dataset, a new method, or a new theoretical claim. The one genuinely new empirical observation, the capture-exceeds-correctness pattern, is itself undermined as a strong, quotable finding by the incommensurability problem raised in Methodology Issue 1. If that comparison is tightened to a defensible, formally comparable statistic (as recommended), the finding would be stronger and more clearly novel; as currently presented, a skeptical reviewer could reasonably ask whether this paper's contribution would be missed from the literature if it did not exist, given that the companion framework article already reports a compressed version of the same pattern (Discussion Section 5.5, acknowledged by the authors themselves).

2. **[Major]** This manuscript is one of at least eight closely related manuscripts from the same research programme built on overlapping or directly shared underlying data and code (`M2`, `M2.3`, `M3`, `M4`, `M5`, and, per the Discussion's own reference to "Objective 2," at least one more). The manuscript explicitly positions itself as a "companion" to `M2.3` and states plainly that `M2.3` "already reports a compressed, three-line version of this pattern." A journal reviewer evaluating this submission independently, without access to the internal project history that makes the distinction clear to the authors, would be entitled to ask whether this is legitimate follow-on work or a division of one publishable unit into two papers to increase publication count. The manuscript's own non-duplication argument (Discussion Section 5.5) is reasonable in substance, but it needs to be made more forcefully and, ideally, supported by a concrete demonstration that the two papers' reviewer pools and use cases genuinely differ, for example by naming the kind of reader (a computational-methods audience wanting benchmark depth) who would read this paper but not need to read `M2.3`, and vice versa. As it stands, the risk of a salami-slicing objection from an editor or reviewer is real and should be pre-empted more directly in the cover letter and in the paper's own framing, not left to a single Discussion subsection.

3. **[Minor]** The five research objectives stated in `O3/proposal.normalized.json` and reflected in the paper's structure are almost entirely about constructing infrastructure (a taxonomy, an evidence table, a scale comparison) rather than about testing a hypothesis about groundwater systems. This is defensible for a methods/benchmarking paper, but the paper would benefit from stating, at least once, what a groundwater practitioner (someone who does not care about HydroSheaf specifically) would do differently after reading this paper. The closest the paper comes is Discussion Section 5.4's monitoring-design paragraph, which is a genuine practical implication; that paragraph should be expanded and could reasonably be promoted higher in the Discussion, since it is the paper's strongest claim to practical significance outside the HydroSheaf project itself.

## Cross-Section Consistency Audit

The following checks were performed systematically across the manuscript.

**Numerical consistency.** All headline numbers appearing in the Abstract (edge recall 0.845, edge precision 0.487, age reportability 0.259, age within-factor-2 0.564, reaction recall 0.599, reaction precision 0.639) were checked against Table 2 and Section 4.2 and matched exactly. The calibration-gap numbers in Section 4.3 (log10 R2 0.482 to 0.962; MRS 48.9% versus 25.0% chance) matched Table 3 and Figure 3 exactly. The benchmark-scale numbers in Section 4.5 (240 scenarios, 21,600 fits, 13 scenarios, 16,536 rows, 12 scenarios, 242 edges) matched Table 4 exactly. No numerical inconsistency was found between the Abstract, Results text, and Tables.

**Terminological consistency.** The terms "capture-type" and "correctness-type" are introduced in Methods Section 3.4 and used identically in Results and Discussion. "Independent," "prior-informed," "calibrated emulation," and "conventional baseline" are used consistently with the definitions given in Section 3.3. No drift was found.

**Claim consistency.** The Conclusion (Section 6) does not introduce any claim, number, or finding that was not already presented in Results or Discussion. The Abstract's claims are a strict subset of the Discussion's claims. This is a genuine strength.

**Methodological consistency.** One inconsistency was found: Section 3.2 describes the age-layer graph-regularization design as comparing "single-node age estimates against weak/medium/strong graph-regularized estimates," but Results Section 4.4 and Table 1 report only a single randomized-control comparison and a single "smallest candidate improvement" figure, with no breakdown by weak/medium/strong regularization strength anywhere in the Results, Table 1, or Figure 4. Either the weak/medium/strong breakdown should be reported (even briefly) in Results, or the Methods description should be simplified to match what is actually reported, since as written a reader is told to expect a three-way comparison that never appears.

## Research Integrity Red Flag Scan

No evidence of data fabrication, image manipulation, or statistically impossible results was found. The following observations were made and are reported per the mandatory scan protocol.

**Statistical red flags.** Not applicable in the conventional sense (no p-values are reported anywhere in the manuscript, which is consistent with the benchmark-comparison design but means the usual p-hacking checks do not apply). No implausibly precise or implausibly clean numbers were found; several reported values (for example, 0.618 F1 recurring across three diagnostic topology variants) are identical because the variants are, by the authors' own account, minor post-processing differences on the same underlying inference, not independent measurements, which is a legitimate explanation rather than a red flag, though see Tables and Figures Issue 2 above for a presentation concern this raises.

**Reporting red flags.** No evidence of selective reporting was found; the paper reports negative and unfavorable results prominently, including the age layer's non-independent scenario outperforming its independent scenarios (Section 4.4) and the topology layer's near-zero negative-control results (Table 1). This transparency about unfavorable or complicating results is a genuine strength and is called out explicitly here because it is not common.

**HARKing.** Not applicable in the traditional sense; the paper does not present a priori hypotheses, and its stated objectives (per `O3/proposal.normalized.json`) are constructive (build a taxonomy, quantify a pattern) rather than predictive. No evidence that the central finding was used to reverse-engineer a hypothesis was found, since the paper does not claim to have predicted the pattern in advance and states this directly in `O3/Outline.md`'s claim guardrails ("A claim that the cross-component pattern was predicted in advance... must not appear").

**Data integrity indicators.** The underlying data are not independently verifiable by this reviewer because the repository DOI in the Code and Data Availability statement is an explicit placeholder (`10.5281/zenodo.PLACEHOLDER-DOI`). This is disclosed openly by the authors as a pre-submission gap rather than concealed, which is the correct practice, but it means every number in this review's verification ledger below is Unverifiable in the strict sense (I could not independently pull the source CSV files) and I have relied on the manuscript's own internal consistency and the authors' documented DECISIONS.md provenance trail rather than independent recomputation.

**Adversarial questions.** The single assumption that would collapse the paper's main conclusion is the validity of comparing three non-identical statistics on one axis (Methodology Issue 1); if a reader rejects that framing, the "recall exceeds precision in all three layers" claim reduces to three separate, unremarkable observations about three separate benchmarks. ## Critical Flaw Assessment

**Condition 1, critical methodology flaw.** The incommensurability of the three "capture/correctness" statistics (Methodology Issue 1) is a real weakness, but it does not meet the bar for this override, because the flaw is disclosed by the authors themselves, is fixable through revision (either narrowing the claim to genuinely comparable statistics or adding a rigorous treatment of the incommensurability), and correcting it would refine rather than reverse the paper's conclusions: even a strictly apples-to-apples version of the comparison would very likely still show topology's precision-recall gap and reaction's near-parity, based on the underlying numbers already reported. This is a major revision item, not a fatal flaw.

**Condition 2, unreliable conclusions.** The conclusions drawn (a recurring capture-over-correctness pattern; a rigor asymmetry between the two calibration exercises; uneven benchmark scale and field-transfer scope) are each supported by the specific numbers presented for them, and the paper is unusually careful, by the standard of most manuscripts this reviewer has seen, about stating what its numbers do and do not establish (see the Research Integrity Red Flag Scan's note on transparency about unfavorable results). The conclusions do not extend meaningfully beyond what the evidence shows. No override triggered here.

**Condition 3, insufficient contribution.** This is the closest call. The paper's contribution, by its own account, is synthesis and bookkeeping over already-reported material, and the one new empirical claim is weakened by the methodological issues above. However, the paper does perform genuine, non-trivial work: it identifies a real, previously unstated pattern across three independently built benchmarks, provides a working taxonomy that could be reused, and is honest about the limits of its own comparison in a way that adds value beyond just restating three existing results side by side. ## Summary for the authors

This manuscript does something genuinely useful: it takes three benchmark efforts that were built separately, for separate purposes, on separate timetables, and asks a question none of the three individual papers could ask on their own, whether they behave alike under a common evaluation logic. The answer it finds, that recall-type performance exceeds precision-type performance under independent evaluation in all three layers, is a real and interesting observation, and the paper is unusually honest about its own limits: it flags a code-staleness risk it did not have to flag, it reports a scenario where a non-independent result outperforms every independent one without burying it, and it explicitly declines to claim the pattern was predicted in advance. That kind of transparency is rare and should be preserved through revision, not sacrificed for a punchier story.

The paper is not yet ready, for three connected reasons. First, the central comparison rests on three statistics that the Methods section itself admits are not the same kind of thing, and the paper needs to either narrow its claim to a genuinely comparable statistic or do the harder work of characterizing exactly how and why the three metrics can still be compared meaningfully; right now the disclosure is honest but not sufficient. Second, every number in the paper is reported as a bare point estimate, when the source data in at least one layer already carries confidence intervals; a paper built entirely on comparing the relative size of numbers needs to show whether those numbers are distinguishable from noise. Third, the paper needs to confront more directly, and earlier, the question a reviewer will ask on sight: why is this a separate paper from the companion framework article that already reports a compressed version of the same finding? The Discussion has the right instinct here but makes the case too late and too briefly for what is likely to be the first objection an editor raises.

None of this requires starting over. The underlying comparison is sound in spirit, the writing is clear, and the transparency already built into this project's culture, evident throughout the `DECISIONS.md` trail, is exactly the right instinct for exactly this kind of paper. What is needed is a tighter, more defensible central statistic, honest uncertainty bounds, a stronger and earlier defense of the paper's independent standing, and a completed data repository before this goes anywhere near a journal.
