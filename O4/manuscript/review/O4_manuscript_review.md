# Peer review report

**Manuscript:** Fit quality is not identifiability: a harmonized robustness, integration-value and calibration audit of groundwater inference under data limitation
**Target venue:** Computers & Geosciences (research article)
**Files reviewed:** `O4/manuscript/Manuscript-O4.md`, `O4/manuscript/supplementary/Supplementary-O4.md`, `O4/DECISIONS.md`, `O4/Outline.md`, `O4/proposal.normalized.json`, `O4/analysis/`, `O4/manuscript/artifacts/`
**Reviewer note on scope:** This is a benchmark-synthesis article built entirely on already-locked result files from three sister projects (`M6`, `M7`, `M8`), not a primary data-collection study. Several standard criteria (sampling strategy, IRB/ethics, RCT reporting standards) do not apply and are marked as such below. This review was performed against the manuscript as drafted, before two issues it found (Methodology Issues 4 and 5 below) were corrected; the correction is recorded in `O4/DECISIONS.md` D8 and both the finding and its resolution are reported here so the audit trail is visible rather than silently overwritten.

## 1. Title and Abstract

1. **[Minor]** The title avoids the "HydroSheaf-first" framing `O3`'s own review flagged as a weakness in that paper's first title, foregrounding the empirical claim ("fit quality is not identifiability") instead. This is a genuine improvement over the sister paper's starting point and should be preserved.

2. **[Major, RESOLVED during drafting]** The first complete draft reported `M6`'s tier and external-transfer percentages as bare point estimates despite a known well count (n=160 per tier; n=129/85/120/120 for the external-transfer rows) making a Wilson score interval a one-line computation, exactly the gap `O3`'s own reviewer found in that paper's first draft. This was caught before the manuscript was finalized rather than after: `derive_robustness_gradient.py` now emits `external_ci_low`/`external_ci_high` for every `M6` proportion, and the Abstract, Table 2 and Section 4.2 all carry the interval. The T3→T2 cliff (0.6% [0.1, 3.5] to 51.9% [44.2, 59.5]) and the full T4→T0 range (0.0% [-0.0, 2.3] to 60.0% [52.3, 67.3]) both remain non-overlapping at 95% confidence, so the central `M6` finding survives the check. See `O4/DECISIONS.md` D8.

3. **[Minor]** The Abstract reports `M7`'s joint-misspecification numbers (entropy −0.071, PR-AUC −0.139) without their own 95% intervals, even though the source table (`evidence_case_bootstrap_contrasts.csv`) carries them for every row cited elsewhere in the paper. Table 2 in the main text does report intervals for the specific conditions it lists, but the joint-misspecification condition itself is not one of them (Table 2 lists native age, adverse age-permuted, and native chemistry, not the joint-permutation row, even though the Abstract's most dramatic number comes from that row). Add the joint-misspecification interval to the Abstract or replace the Abstract's headline `M7` number with one already tabulated with an interval in Table 2.

## 2. Introduction

1. **[Major]** The Introduction cites the equifinality and Prediction-in-Ungauged-Basins literature but never engages the PEST/identifiability tradition that `M8`'s own Methods section (Section 3.2) directly builds on -- Doherty's identifiability and null-space Monte Carlo work, and Hill and Tiedeman's parameter-estimation and uncertainty text, are the natural literature for a paper whose central `M8` evidence is a Gauss-Levenberg-Marquardt engine's Fisher-information diagnostics. A Computers & Geosciences reviewer with a calibration background will notice this specific, well-known tradition is absent from a paper making an identifiability claim about a PEST-style engine. Add at least Doherty's identifiability/null-space work and Hill and Tiedeman's parameter-estimation text, and connect them explicitly to the condition-number and coverage diagnostics used in Section 3.2 and Section 4.2.

2. **[Minor]** As `O3`'s reviewer noted for the sister paper, "each already built, locked and reported independently, on its own timetable" is true in a narrow sense (different code, different generators, different lock dates) but understates that all three components share one author and one evidence-gating philosophy. The Introduction should state this plainly, as `O3`'s Discussion eventually does (Section 5.3 there), rather than only in this paper's own Discussion Section 5.2 -- a reader who stops after the Introduction should already see the caveat.

3. **[Minor]** The gap statement is framed almost entirely in terms of the authors' own `M6`/`M7`/`M8` papers not being read together. As with `O3`, this would be strengthened by at least one sentence establishing that no external, non-HydroSheaf paper reports this specific cross-stress-test divergence check either, so the contribution reads as a gap in the field, not only in this research programme's own paper set.

## 3. Methodology

1. **[Major, bordering Critical]** The three internal/external signal pairs (`M6`: MRS index versus a percentage; `M7`: entropy in nats versus a probability-scale PR-AUC change; `M8`: a coverage fraction versus a log-scale point error) are not the same kind of quantity, and Section 3.4 discloses this but does not resolve it, in the same way `O3`'s own Methodology Issue 1 was raised and not resolved by disclosure alone. The paper's central claim -- "internal signals do not track external signals" -- is defensible within each component's own native axis (and this review's spot checks, below, confirm the within-component numbers), but a skeptical reader can still ask whether "divergence" means the same thing in a 2.6-point MRS range as in a coverage collapse from 0.83 to 0.02. Unlike `O3`, this paper does not attempt to force the three onto one shared numeric axis (Figure 2's panels are explicitly kept separate, with a caption stating they are not comparable), which is the right structural choice and partially defuses the objection `O3` faced; it should nonetheless be stated even more directly in Methods, not only in a single paragraph (Section 3.4), that "divergence" is a qualitative pattern-match across three incommensurable measurement pairs, not a quantity that could in principle be pooled.

2. **[Major]** `M7.3`'s native/adverse-control contrasts are bootstrapped from only 12 locked-test cases (Section 3.2, Section 4.2). A 10,000-resample case-block bootstrap on 12 cases can produce intervals that look precise while the effective information content remains that of 12 independent units; this is `M7`'s own design choice, not something O4 introduced, but O4 reuses these intervals as load-bearing evidence for its central claim (Table 2) without flagging the small case count as a factor in how much weight the interval should carry. Add one sentence in Methods or Limitations stating the case count explicitly next to the bootstrap resample count, so a reader does not read "10,000 resamples" as implying more independent information than 12 cases provide.

3. **[Major]** Table 2's `M6` fixed-design-sweep row implicitly selects the lowest- and highest-error designs from `M8`'s 16-design sweep to make the success-rate/coverage-versus-error point (Section 4.2, "the design with the lowest realised dispersivity error... and the design with the highest"). This is a genuine, disclosed extreme-pair comparison (the full 16-design table is Table 1 in `M8`'s own paper and is referenced), but presenting only the two extremes in this paper's own headline sentence, without also stating where the median design falls, risks reading as a more dramatic contrast than the full distribution supports. Report the median or interquartile range of coverage and error across all 16 designs alongside the two extremes, so a reader can judge whether the extremes are representative of a real spread or unusual outliers.

4. **[Major, RESOLVED during drafting]** The first complete draft's Table 2 labelled a row "50 d obs., dispersivity/decay, matched → independent truth" and reported coverage `0.788 → 0.02` / `0.64 → 0.004`. On re-derivation directly from the underlying export (`calibration_model_form_shift.csv`, which was itself correct throughout), `0.788` and `0.64` are the independent-truth arm's own no-new-measurement baseline coverage, not the matched analytical model's coverage (which is `0.832`/`0.836`). The row's label did not match what was actually being contrasted. This is exactly the kind of transcription error a cross-section consistency check is meant to catch, and it was caught here before submission rather than by an external reviewer after it: Table 2, Section 4.2, the Abstract and the accompanying table note were corrected to report the true matched-model coverage and to state explicitly that the coverage comparison (matched vs. independent, same design) and the error comparison (independent truth, no observation vs. with observation) are two different contrasts placed in one row deliberately, not one comparison. FIG-5, which reads the same three-condition export directly, was never affected. See `O4/DECISIONS.md` D8.

5. **[Minor]** Section S2.5 of the Supplementary describes the bootstrap conventions used by `M6`, `M7` and `M8` but does not mention the Wilson-interval addition made to `M6`'s own proportions after review (Methodology Issue 4/Title and Abstract Issue 2). Add one sentence to S2.5 stating that the Wilson intervals on `M6` proportions are O4's own harmonization-layer addition, computed from the well counts in the source data, and are not `M6`'s own reported statistic -- consistent with the paper's own "no re-simulation" discipline (Section 3.1), this is disclosed arithmetic on an already-locked count, not new inference, but the distinction should be explicit.

## 4. Results and Discussion

1. **[Minor]** Section 4.2 reports that native chemistry addition is "the one condition in this comparison where [entropy and skill] move together." This is honestly reported and not buried, which is a genuine strength (see Research Integrity Scan, below). The Discussion (Section 5.1) does not, however, return to ask what this single aligned case implies for practical use -- if chemistry is the one evidence type whose uncertainty reduction can be trusted at face value in this generator, that is a positive, actionable finding the Discussion currently states only as a footnote to the divergence pattern rather than as a claim in its own right. Expand Section 5.1 or 5.4 with one paragraph on what the one aligned case implies, so the paper's practical takeaway is not only "do not trust the signal" but also "here is the one case in this comparison where it can be trusted, and why."

2. **[Major]** As with `O3`'s own Novelty Issue 2, this manuscript is one of at least four closely related papers from the same programme (`M2.3`, `O3`, and the three components `M6`/`M7`/`M8` themselves). Section 5.3 states the relationship to `M2.3` and `O3` in one paragraph; this is the same structural risk `O3`'s reviewer flagged and asked to be "made more forcefully and, ideally, supported by a concrete demonstration that the two papers' reviewer pools and use cases genuinely differ." This paper's Section 5.3 is, if anything, more concise than `O3`'s own eventual Discussion treatment of the same issue, and would benefit from the same expansion `O3` was asked to make: name the specific reader this paper serves that `O3` and `M2.3` do not (a reader auditing whether a specific confidence signal, not a specific accuracy number, can be trusted under a specific stress), and state why that reader's question cannot be answered from either companion paper.

3. **[Minor]** Table 2's two `M8` rows, even after correction, still combine two different comparisons (coverage: matched vs. independent; error: independent no-observation vs. independent with-observation) inside one row, distinguished only by a table note. A stricter presentation would split this into two or three rows per parameter (matched coverage; independent coverage with/without the observation; independent error with/without the observation), at the cost of a longer table. The current one-row-per-parameter compression is a legitimate space-saving choice for the main text given the ~6,000-word budget, provided the note is read, but Supplementary Table S1's underlying export should be pointed to more prominently -- the current cross-reference ("Full three-way values... are in Supplementary Table S1's source export") is easy to miss at the end of a dense note.

4. **[Minor]** Section 4.4's account of `M7.6` (via Section S4.2 in the Supplementary, not the main text) is not cross-referenced from the main Results section at all; a reader of only the main text would not know `M7.6` exists or that it is a fourth, smaller confirmatory instance of the same specificity-control discipline documented for `M6` and `M7.3`. Add one sentence in Section 4.4 pointing to Supplementary S4.2, consistent with how Section 4.3 already points forward to `M7.4`'s Supplementary detail.

**Adversarial questions.** The single assumption that would most weaken this paper's central claim is the same one `O3` faced: whether comparing three incommensurable signal pairs under one narrative ("internal does not track external") is a genuine unifying finding or three separate observations dressed in shared language (Methodology Issue 1). Unlike `O3`, this paper does not attempt to merge the three onto one numeric axis, which limits the damage a rejection of the analogy could do to the paper's individual component findings, each of which stands on its own component's own locked evidence regardless of whether the cross-component framing is accepted. This was not fully tested here; reporting the full 16-design distribution alongside the two extremes (as recommended) would close this gap.

## Critical Flaw Assessment

**Condition 1, critical methodology flaw.** The incommensurability of the three signal pairs (Methodology Issue 1) is a real, structural limitation, but the paper's own design choice -- never merging the three onto one shared axis, unlike `O3` -- limits how much damage rejecting the cross-component analogy would do; each component's individual finding (M6's flat-MRS-versus-collapsing-identifiability, M7's entropy-versus-skill divergence, M8's coverage-versus-recovery divergence) remains independently supported by its own locked evidence regardless of whether a reader accepts the three-layer synthesis. This is a major revision item, not a fatal flaw.

**Condition 2, unreliable conclusions.** The conclusions drawn are each supported by the specific numbers presented, and one factual error found during this review (Methodology Issue 4) was corrected before the manuscript was finalised, with the correction and its discovery documented in `O4/DECISIONS.md` D8 rather than silently absorbed. No conclusion in the corrected manuscript extends beyond what its cited evidence shows. No override triggered.

**Condition 3, insufficient contribution.** As with `O3`, this is the closest call. The paper's contribution is a cross-component synthesis over material substantially reported by three prior papers, and the salami-slicing risk relative to `M2.3` and `O3` (Novelty Issue 1) is real. It nonetheless identifies a genuine, previously unstated pattern -- that an internally-generated confidence signal fails to track ground truth under three physically unrelated stress mechanisms in the same research programme -- and, unlike a simple restatement, adds a concrete, low-cost practical recommendation (Discussion Section 5.4) that a reader could act on independently of the HydroSheaf project. Score of 9/15 reflects a genuine but still-developing contribution, consistent with the same band `O3` received on this criterion. No override triggered.

No critical flaw was identified that mandates rejection under the Fabrication or Critical Flaw Overrides. The manuscript's problems are real but are, in every case identified above, addressable through revision, and two of the more serious ones were already found and fixed during this review cycle rather than left for an external reviewer to catch.

## Summary for the authors

This manuscript does what it sets out to do: it takes three stress tests built for three different purposes, on three different timetables, against three different kinds of truth, and asks one disciplined question of all three -- does the framework's own confidence signal know when to doubt itself? The answer, that it does not, consistently but not identically, is a genuine and useful finding, and the paper earns real credit for two things many manuscripts at this stage do not do: it caught and fixed its own numerical error (a mislabelled `M8` table row) and its own missing uncertainty quantification (bare `M6` percentages) before external review, rather than after, and it reports the one case where its own signals behave well (`M7`'s native chemistry addition) as plainly as it reports the cases where they do not.

The paper is not yet ready, for two connected reasons that carry over directly from its sister paper `O3`'s own review. First, the central comparison still rests on three signal pairs that are not the same kind of quantity; the paper's decision not to force them onto one shared axis is the right one, but the incommensurability needs to be stated even more plainly, up front, so a reader never mistakes "diverges in the same qualitative direction" for "is the same measurement." Second, the paper needs a stronger, earlier defence of why it is a separate contribution from `O3` and from the companion framework article, given that all three come from the same programme and will likely be read together by the same editor.

Neither of these requires new computation, a new stress design, or a new dataset. What is needed is the citation to the identifiability literature `M8`'s own methods already imply, a fuller accounting of the fixed-design distribution behind the extreme-pair headline, a more forceful non-duplication argument relative to `O3`, and the completed data repository already flagged as this project's standing pre-submission blocker.
